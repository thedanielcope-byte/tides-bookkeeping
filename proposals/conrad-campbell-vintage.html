Where you’re starting

The business is growing. The system underneath it isn’t keeping up.

You celebrated a year on Whatnot in August. You run two shows a week, sometimes three, and move about 200 pieces a week. Shows run from $1,700 to over $5,000, and your goal is $3,000 every time you go live. Viewers have climbed since January and haven’t stopped.

Your record-keeping isn’t the problem. Every purchase goes on a card with a receipt, and you keep hard copies. The problem is the system. My Reseller Genie was supposed to tie it all together. Instead you’re 20–30 hours into deep dives, you added up every January transaction by hand, and you’ve started turning down new income because you don’t know how to track it. The plan below moves the books to QuickBooks Online, rebuilds 2026 from January 1, and has you running on a clean weekly routine by October 1.

What we found

Four things to fix, and to build in going forward.

01
August shows $3,000 more in sales than Whatnot paid
My Reseller Genie reports about $3,000 more in August sales than Whatnot’s own monthly statement. Most months the gap is a few hundred. Overstated sales means paying estimated tax on money you never received. The rebuild ties every month to Whatnot’s statement and your bank deposits, pins down where the gap comes from, and gives you concrete numbers to take back to Whatnot if it’s on their side.
02
Thousands of transactions, when you need one line a week
Whatnot’s reports pack commission, payment processing, shipping and tax into one gross number, and tracking them item by item buries you in 30-cent entries. Instead, each week becomes one clean entry: sales, then each fee, then what landed in the bank. You can see last week against the week before, and whether a show did any good.
03
Whatnot collects the sales tax, but the return is still yours
You already report Whatnot as the third party collecting on your behalf. Washington’s B&O tax still applies to your gross sales, and as the Squarespace store grows, those sales are yours to collect on and report by location. Add quarterly estimated taxes on the federal side, and it pays to have every number in its own place before the deadline.
04
New income streams need a home before you say yes
Affiliate payouts through Impact are a marketing cost, and they don’t show up on the Whatnot report at all. Brand deals are a different kind of income, taxed differently in Washington. Both get their own categories from day one, so you can stop turning down deals just because you don’t know where they’d go.

Scope of work

Set it up right, then keep it right.

  • Cleanup and QuickBooks build (one-time). QuickBooks Online set up with a chart of accounts built for a vintage reseller. Whatnot, website, affiliate commissions and brand deals each get their own category, along with inventory and cost of goods, and Gusto is connected. When you start bringing pieces in from Italy and the Middle East, freight and duty go into the cost of each piece. Opening balances come from your 2025 return. Then every transaction from January 1 through September 30 is rebuilt from Whatnot’s monthly statements and your bank records, and reconciled. That’s how we find the August gap.
  • Weekly bookkeeping, closed every month. Each week’s Whatnot activity is in the books by Friday, with sales, fees, shipping and tax split out and each payout matched to its deposit, local pickups included. Your bank, card and Gusto payroll are reconciled, cost of goods is posted, and the month closes with a P&L you can read in five minutes. You run the shows. The books run without you.
  • Washington, estimated taxes, and your CPA. B&O and sales tax each sit in their own bucket, with your quarterly Washington return numbers ready to file, facilitator deduction included. Or I can file it for you for $50 a quarter. Quarterly figures go out for your estimated taxes, and your CPA gets a clean year-end package. I don’t replace your CPA; I’m the monthly engine that feeds them. As you grow, we’ll bring the S-corp question to them with real numbers.
