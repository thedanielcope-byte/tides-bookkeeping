Where you’re starting

A fast launch, and a blank page to build the books on.

You opened on August 16 and did roughly $37,000 on 111 orders in the first month — well ahead of plan. The books are a blank page: Shopify, one Mercury account, one Mercury card. That is the best starting position there is. Most bookkeeping starts by unwinding someone else’s decisions; here there’s nothing to undo.

What’s coming is what makes the timing matter. Marsh & Tide goes to pre-order at $599, the dealer network opens to boutiques, and the plan is five to ten drops a year — each one a big inventory buy months before the cash from it clears. The books need to be built for that model now, not reconstructed from saved emails at tax time.

What we found

Four things to build in from the start.

01
Pre-orders are owed product, not profit yet
Most of the $37,000 from launch month is pre-order cash for sets that haven’t shipped. Booked as sales, the P&L looks rich the month you launch and takes a hit the month the pallets land. Tracked as customer deposits, you can see exactly how much of the Mercury balance is already spoken for — the number that matters most with Marsh & Tide at roughly 1,000 sets.
02
$30–40k of opening stock went on personal cards
The business was funded before the Mercury account existed. Recorded properly, that becomes owner funding the business can pay back to you on a schedule you choose, and the stock it bought lands in inventory and cost of goods, where it belongs.
03
Shopify deposits are net, not sales
Every payout arrives with processing fees, pay-later costs, refunds and sales tax already taken out. Coded as one lump, revenue is understated and the tax you collected looks like money you get to keep. Split properly, with cost of goods posted per SKU, your 52–54% margin becomes a number you can see by collection — not an estimate.
04
The dealer network changes how you get paid
Boutiques pay on invoice and terms, not at checkout. Wholesale runs out of QuickBooks — invoices and purchase orders, each payment matched to Mercury automatically, a live view of who owes you what, and a resale certificate on file for every dealer.

Scope of work

Set it up right, then keep it right.

  • Catch-up and QuickBooks build (one-time). QuickBooks Online Plus and A2X connected to Shopify and Mercury. A chart of accounts built for a product brand — direct and wholesale sales split apart, inventory, customer deposits for pre-orders, and freight and duty carried into the cost of each set. The stock you bought on personal cards recorded as owner funding. Then every transaction from May 26 through September 30 recorded and reconciled, so October starts clean.
  • Weekly bookkeeping, closed every month. Shopify payouts, Mercury and the Mercury card reconciled, pre-order deposits released to sales as orders ship, cost of goods posted per SKU, and a month-end close with a P&L you can read in five minutes. You have a full-time job and a garage full of pallets — the books run without you.
  • CPA-ready from day one. You don’t have a CPA yet, and you’ll want one before year-end. I can introduce you to CPAs I work with, and you’ll walk in with clean numbers and the right questions already on the table: how to treat the money you put in, whether an S-corp election makes sense and when, and the cleanest way to pay your sons.
