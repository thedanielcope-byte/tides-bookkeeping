📍 About North Dakota

Powered by the Bakken.

North Dakota's economy is defined by energy and agriculture. The Bakken formation in the western part of the state made North Dakota one of the nation's leading oil and gas producers, and severance taxes on that production help fund state government and keep other taxes low. Agriculture is the other backbone — the state is a national leader in wheat, canola, flaxseed, sunflowers, and dry edible beans — alongside a broader energy and manufacturing base.

The ND tax picture

North Dakota is one of the lowest-tax states in the country, and oil is the reason why. Revenue from the state's oil extraction and gross production taxes (each around 5% of value) lets North Dakota keep its personal income tax remarkably low: it's graduated with just a few brackets, the lowest of which is taxed at 0%, and a top rate of only about 2.5% (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). The state sales tax is 5%, with cities and counties adding local rates on top. Corporations face a graduated corporate income tax topping out around 4.3%. For most small business owners, North Dakota's income tax burden is among the lightest in the nation.
