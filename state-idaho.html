📍 About Idaho

Micron, Potatoes, and the Boise Boom.

Idaho is one of the fastest-growing states in the country, and its economy is far more diverse than its potato reputation suggests. Boise anchors a growing technology sector led by Micron Technology, a global memory-chip maker headquartered there, alongside food processing, manufacturing, and healthcare. Agriculture remains huge — Idaho leads the nation in potato production and is a major dairy and sugar-beet producer — and tourism, outdoor recreation, and lumber round out an economy fueled by steady in-migration from higher-cost states.

The ID tax picture

Idaho keeps things relatively simple: it levies a flat 5.3% individual income tax (as of 2026 — confirm the current rate before filing), down from a graduated system after recent cuts. The state sales tax is 6%, and unlike many states Idaho applies it to groceries — though residents can claim a grocery tax credit to offset it. Local sales taxes are rare, limited mostly to resort and tourist towns that levy a local-option tax, so the combined rate is close to the 6% state rate in most of the state. Overall, Idaho's tax burden is moderate and its rules are straightforward for most businesses.
