📍 About Illinois

Chicago and the Prairie State's Trading Floors.

Illinois has one of the largest and most diversified economies in the Midwest, anchored by Chicago — a global center for finance, futures and derivatives trading (the CME Group), and corporate headquarters. The state is a national transportation and logistics hub, with O'Hare International Airport and a dense rail network moving freight across the country. Downstate, Illinois is an agricultural powerhouse and a top producer of corn and soybeans, and home to major manufacturers and food and pharmaceutical companies, from Deere & Company in the Quad Cities to Abbott and AbbVie in the Chicago suburbs.

The IL tax picture

Illinois pairs a flat income tax with some of the highest property and sales taxes in the country. The individual income tax is a flat 4.95% (as of 2026 — confirm the current rate before filing), and businesses organized as corporations or partnerships also owe a Personal Property Replacement Tax on top of that. The state sales-tax base is 6.25%, but local add-ons push the combined average near 9%, and Chicago's 10.25% is among the highest big-city rates in the nation. Illinois property taxes are also among the highest anywhere — a major cost for business owners who own real estate. The flat income rate is simple; the surrounding tax burden is not.
