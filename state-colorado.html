📍 About Colorado

The Centennial State's Mile-High Economy.

Colorado has a diverse, high-growth economy. The Denver-Boulder corridor is a major technology and startup hub, and the state ranks among the nation's leaders in aerospace and defense, with a heavy federal and satellite presence along the Front Range. Add tourism and outdoor recreation built around world-class ski resorts, a large craft-brewing industry, energy production, agriculture on the eastern plains, and a pioneering legal-cannabis sector, and you get one of the most dynamic economies in the Mountain West.

The CO tax picture

Colorado combines a flat income tax with an unusually low state sales-tax base — and a famously complicated local layer. The individual income tax is a flat 4.4% (as of 2026 — confirm the current rate before filing). The state sales-tax rate is just 2.9%, one of the lowest in the nation, but Colorado is a home-rule state: many cities collect and administer their own sales taxes separately, so combined rates average around 7.9% and vary widely (Denver is about 8.8%). That patchwork of self-collecting jurisdictions makes Colorado sales-tax compliance surprisingly complex. TABOR, the state's Taxpayer's Bill of Rights, can also trigger taxpayer refunds in surplus years.
