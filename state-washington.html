📍 About Washington

No Income Tax, Home of Big Tech.

Washington is a technology and aerospace powerhouse - home to Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing, Starbucks, and Costco - clustered around the Seattle-Bellevue metro. The state also leads in aerospace manufacturing, global trade through its deepwater ports, and agriculture (apples, wine, and wheat), with a fast-growing clean-energy sector. Its lack of a personal income tax and heavy concentration of high-wage tech jobs shape both its economy and its unusual tax structure.

The WA tax picture

Washington has no personal income tax, but that does not mean no business tax. The state's signature Business & Occupation (B&O) tax is levied on gross receipts - total revenue, with no deduction for costs or losses - at rates that vary by activity (for example, roughly 0.471% for retailing, 0.484% for manufacturing and wholesaling, and about 1.5% or more for services, as of 2026 - confirm current rate). Sales tax is also high: 6.5% state plus local rates that push combined totals to roughly 9% to 10.35%, with Seattle near the top. Washington additionally imposes a 7% excise tax on long-term capital gains above a threshold (around $270,000, with a higher rate on very large gains, as of 2026 - confirm current rate and threshold), and there is no corporate income tax.
