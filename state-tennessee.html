📍 About Tennessee

A State Built for Business.

With ~7.1 million residents, no state income tax on wages, and a diverse economy spanning healthcare, music, automotive, and logistics, Tennessee is one of the fastest-growing states in the country — with a distinct regional character across its three grand divisions, from the Mississippi River to the Great Smoky Mountains.

🎵
Middle Tennessee
Nashville · Murfreesboro · Franklin · Clarksville
The economic engine of TN — anchored by Nashville's healthcare HQs (HCA, Community Health Systems), the global music industry, state government, and the country's fastest-growing metro. Includes Vanderbilt, Nissan in Smyrna, GM in Spring Hill, and Fort Campbell.
⛰️
East Tennessee
Knoxville · Chattanooga · Tri-Cities · Pigeon Forge
The Appalachian region — home to the University of Tennessee, the Volkswagen plant in Chattanooga, Eastman Chemical in Kingsport, Dollywood in Pigeon Forge, and the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, the most-visited national park in America.
🌊
West Tennessee
Memphis · Jackson · Germantown · Collierville
Anchored by Memphis on the Mississippi River — the FedEx world hub, the birthplace of blues and rock and roll (Sun Records, Stax, Graceland), and a deep logistics, distribution, and agricultural economy stretching across the cotton and soybean country of the Mississippi Delta.
🥃
Tennessee Whiskey Country
Lynchburg · Tullahoma · Gatlinburg
Tennessee whiskey is a federally recognized category — Jack Daniel's in Lynchburg (the best-selling American whiskey in the world) and George Dickel in Tullahoma anchor a fast-growing craft distillery and agri-tourism corridor that pulls visitors from every state.

Tennessee's Economy & Business Climate

Tennessee is one of the most tax-friendly states in America — there is no state income tax on wages (the old Hall Tax on dividends and interest was fully repealed January 2021), no inheritance tax, and a business climate that has aggressively recruited automakers and corporate headquarters for decades. The results speak for themselves: Nissan, Volkswagen, GM, FedEx, HCA, Dollar General, Tractor Supply, and Eastman all call Tennessee home.

Tennessee was admitted to the Union in 1796 as the 16th state. Its economic identity has long been shaped by music — Nashville as the global capital of country music and songwriting, Memphis as the home of the blues and the birthplace of rock and roll — but today's Tennessee is equally defined by healthcare HQs, advanced automotive manufacturing, the FedEx logistics empire, and the tourism juggernaut anchored by the Great Smoky Mountains.

📦
FedEx World Hub — Memphis
Memphis International is the second-busiest cargo airport on the planet, and FedEx's global super-hub is headquartered in the city. Tens of thousands of jobs and a deep network of logistics, trucking, and distribution companies orbit the Mid-South operation.
🏥
Healthcare HQ Capital — Nashville
Nashville is the headquarters city for HCA Healthcare (the largest US hospital operator), Community Health Systems (Franklin), and dozens of healthcare-services companies. Combined with Vanderbilt University Medical Center, it forms one of the densest healthcare economies in America.
🚗
Automotive Manufacturing Corridor
Nissan North America's largest US assembly plant is in Smyrna, with its headquarters in Franklin. GM builds Cadillacs in Spring Hill. Volkswagen assembles the Atlas and ID.4 in Chattanooga. A deep supplier base supports tens of thousands of advanced manufacturing jobs.
🏔️
Tourism — Smokies, Dollywood, Music City
Great Smoky Mountains National Park draws ~13M visitors a year — the most of any US national park. Pigeon Forge (Dollywood), Gatlinburg, Nashville's Lower Broadway honky-tonks and the Grand Ole Opry, and Graceland in Memphis together drive one of the strongest tourism economies in the country.
🎓
Universities & Fort Campbell
The University of Tennessee (Knoxville), Vanderbilt (Nashville), MTSU (Murfreesboro), and the University of Memphis collectively educate hundreds of thousands of students. Fort Campbell on the Kentucky line anchors Clarksville's economy as home of the 101st Airborne Division.

Key Interstate & Highway Infrastructure

Tennessee sits at the crossroads of the Southeast — more interstates intersect inside its borders than almost any other state — which is exactly why FedEx, Nissan, VW, and Amazon all chose to anchor distribution and manufacturing here.

I-40 — The Tennessee Backbone
Runs the entire length of the state east-to-west — connecting Memphis, Nashville, and Knoxville. The single most important logistics artery in Tennessee.
I-65 — Nashville's North-South Spine
Connects Nashville south to Huntsville and Birmingham, north to Bowling Green and Louisville. Anchors Brentwood, Franklin, and the booming Williamson County corridor.
I-24 — Nashville to Chattanooga
The diagonal route through Middle Tennessee — Clarksville to Nashville to Murfreesboro to Chattanooga — and one of the fastest-growing commuter corridors in the state.
I-75 — East TN to the Smokies
Connects Chattanooga north through Knoxville to Kentucky. The primary freight and tourism corridor for East Tennessee and the gateway to the Great Smoky Mountains.
I-55 — Memphis to the Delta
Runs through Memphis on its way from Chicago to New Orleans — anchoring West Tennessee's freight network and the Mississippi River logistics economy.
I-81 — Tri-Cities Corridor
Connects Knoxville north through Johnson City, Kingsport, and Bristol into Virginia — the freight spine of the upper East TN economy and Eastman Chemical's home corridor.

TN Landmarks & Cultural Identity

🎤
Grand Ole Opry & Music Row (Nashville)
Nashville is the global capital of country music and one of the largest music publishing centers in the world — the Grand Ole Opry, the Ryman Auditorium, and Music Row anchor a creative economy that includes recording, publishing, touring, and licensing.
🏔️
Great Smoky Mountains National Park
The most-visited national park in the United States — drawing ~13 million annual visitors and powering the entire Sevier County tourism economy of Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge (Dollywood), and Sevierville.
🎸
Graceland, Beale Street & Sun Studio (Memphis)
Memphis is the home of the blues and the birthplace of rock and roll — Graceland (Elvis Presley's estate) is the second-most-visited home in America after the White House, while Beale Street, Sun Studio, and Stax anchor a globally significant music tourism economy.
