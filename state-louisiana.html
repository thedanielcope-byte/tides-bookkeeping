📍 About Louisiana

Ports, Energy, and the Bayou Economy.

Louisiana's economy runs on energy and trade. The lower Mississippi River corridor hosts one of the densest concentrations of oil, gas, and petrochemical operations in the country, and Louisiana's ports (including the Port of South Louisiana) rank among the nation's busiest by tonnage. Seafood, agriculture (sugarcane, rice, and cotton), and a globally recognized tourism and culture economy centered on New Orleans fill out the picture.

The LA tax picture

As of 2026, Louisiana taxes individual income at a flat 3%, a recent reform that replaced its old graduated brackets, though you should confirm the current rate before filing. The real complexity is sales tax: the state rate is 5% (as of 2025), but Louisiana's parishes (its equivalent of counties) and municipalities add substantial local sales taxes, often administered by local parish collectors rather than the state, pushing combined rates into the 9 to 12% range, among the highest average combined rates in the nation. If you sell across parish lines, local sales tax compliance is genuinely complicated.
