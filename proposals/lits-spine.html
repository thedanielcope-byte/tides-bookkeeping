Clean books — and a real number on every project.

Where you're starting

Growing fast, with no books to show for it.

For three years this ran as a side business alongside a full-time role — one contract at a time, handled after hours. That has changed. Revenue is climbing, projects are stacking up, and going into 2027 the whole thing becomes full-time 1099 work.

The books never made that jump. There are no records for 2026, money arrives as a lump sum per contract and goes back out to subcontractors, and six bank accounts are doing nothing to explain where any of it went.

This isn't a revenue problem. It's a structure problem — and structure is the fixable kind. The goal is simple: books that are current and worth trusting, a real number on every project, and a clean handoff to your CPA so 2027 starts on solid ground instead of guesswork.

What we found

Four things to put in order.

01
2026 has no books
QuickBooks was set up and then set aside, so this year has no financial records at all — nine months of income and expenses living only in Chase and Amex statements, with AI-generated summaries standing in for financials.
02
Project profit is invisible
A contract lands as one lump sum, then subcontractors get paid back out of it. Without job-level tracking there's no way to know whether an engagement cleared $50,000 or $15,000 — only that the money moved.
03
Six accounts, no system behind them
Separating money coming in from money going out was the right instinct. But none of those accounts are connected to an accounting platform, so the separation never turns into a report you can read.
04
1099 season is coming
Most of what leaves the business goes to subcontractors. Without W-9s collected up front and vendor payments tracked through the year, January becomes a scramble — right when 2027 needs your full attention.

Scope of work

Get caught up, then stay clean.

  • One-time cleanup (January–September). Build your books in Puzzle from scratch, connect the Chase accounts and the Amex, and categorize and reconcile every 2026 transaction. Every project gets its own classification, so you can read any single engagement on its own or the whole business at once.
  • Ongoing every month. Reconcile every account, categorize every transaction, and deliver clean financials with profit broken out project by project. Vendors tracked all year and W-9s collected at project start, so January filing is a formality rather than a scramble.
  • In step with your CPA. I own the monthly books; your CPA owns the filings and the S-corp call when you're ready to make it — now working from numbers they can trust.
