About the Area

Oil, Salmon and
a Sales Tax That
Stacks.

The Kenai Peninsula Borough runs from Cooper Landing down to Homer and across to Seward, and it holds five incorporated cities. Soldotna is the borough seat, Kenai is the largest city, Homer anchors the south end, and Seward sits on the eastern shore facing Resurrection Bay.

The economy has three legs. Cook Inlet oil and gas - the Nikiski industrial complex, the platforms out in the Inlet, and the service companies that support them. Fishing - the Kenai River king and sockeye runs, the Homer halibut fleet, and the commercial setnetters and driftnetters of Cook Inlet. And tourism, which for a few months a year floods the peninsula with anglers, cruise passengers at Seward, and RVs on the Sterling Highway.

For bookkeeping, one feature dominates everything: the borough sales tax stacks on top of the city rate. Get that wrong and you are under-collecting on every sale.

History & Economic Identity

The Russians got here first - Fort Nikolaevskaia was established at Kenai in 1791, making it one of the oldest permanent settlements in Alaska. But the modern peninsula was made by two things: oil and fish.

The Swanson River oil discovery in 1957 was the first commercially significant oil find in Alaska, and it helped make the case for statehood two years later. Cook Inlet production followed, the platforms went up, and Nikiski became an industrial complex of refining and gas processing. The Inlet is a mature basin now, but it still anchors a substantial oilfield services economy of welders, machinists, boat operators, and logistics firms.

Fish is the other half. The Kenai River produces the largest king salmon on earth - the all-tackle world record, a 97-pounder, came out of it in 1985 - and the sockeye run brings a personal-use dipnet fishery every July that draws thousands of Alaskans to the river mouth. Homer calls itself the Halibut Fishing Capital of the World and has the charter fleet to back it up. Seward has cruise ships and the Alaska SeaLife Center. Between them, the peninsula gets a summer so intense that many businesses genuinely earn their entire year between Memorial Day and Labor Day.

💰 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
But Read This Anyway.

Alaska has no state sales tax and no state personal income tax — which is exactly why so many business owners get Alaska tax wrong. More than 100 local jurisdictions levy their own sales tax, and the rules change borough by borough and city by city.

Kenai Peninsula Borough: The Numbers

The Kenai Peninsula has the most complicated sales tax setup of any borough in Alaska, because the borough rate stacks on top of the city rate. If you sell in more than one town on this peninsula, you are dealing with more than one rate - and most people get it wrong at least once.

Alaska State Sales Tax None
Borough Sales Tax 3% (applies borough-wide)
Kenai (combined) 6% (3% borough + 3% city)
Soldotna (combined) 6% (3% borough + 3% city)
Homer (combined) ~7.85% (3% borough + 4.85% city)
Seward (combined) ~7% (3% borough + 4% city)

Rates shown are as of 2026 and can change. Always confirm the current rate with the taxing jurisdiction before you rely on it. Tides keeps your books tax-ready and works alongside your CPA — we do not file returns for you.

What It Means for Your Books

➕
The borough rate stacks on the city rate
This is the single most important tax fact on the peninsula. The Kenai Peninsula Borough levies 3% borough-wide, and then each city adds its own on top: Kenai and Soldotna add 3% (6% combined), Homer adds 4.85% (about 7.85% combined), and Seward adds 4% (about 7% combined). Sell in Nikiski or Sterling and you collect only the 3% borough rate. Rates are as of 2026 and do change - the City of Soldotna has a rate change scheduled for January 1, 2027 - so confirm with the borough before relying on them.
🗓️
A summer that has to carry twelve months
Charter captains, guides, lodges, and RV parks on this peninsula routinely earn the overwhelming majority of their annual revenue between Memorial Day and Labor Day. That makes deposit handling and deferred revenue a real accounting problem, not a technicality - bookings taken in February for a July trip are not February revenue. We book them properly so your numbers mean something.
🐟
Fisheries taxes are their own animal
If you catch, buy, or process fish, Alaska's fisheries business tax and fishery resource landing tax may apply on top of everything else - and fish tickets, crew shares, and permit costs all need to be handled correctly in the books. We keep the records clean and organized so your CPA can file confidently.
🌐
Shipping into the peninsula from elsewhere? ARSSTC
If you are a remote seller delivering into the Kenai Peninsula - or anywhere else in Alaska that levies a local sales tax - the Alaska Remote Sellers Sales Tax Commission (ARSSTC) is the single point of registration and filing across participating jurisdictions. The threshold is $100,000 in gross statewide sales in the current or prior calendar year. The old 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025, so transaction count no longer matters.
