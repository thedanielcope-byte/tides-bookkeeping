📍 About North Carolina

A State Built for Business.

North Carolina is one of the largest, most diverse state economies in the Southeast — with a distinct regional character spanning the Blue Ridge Mountains, the booming Piedmont (Charlotte, Triad, Triangle), and the Atlantic Coastal Plain.

⛰️
The Mountains
Asheville · Boone · Hickory · Hendersonville
Western NC's tourism-driven economy — anchored by Asheville's craft brewery scene, the Biltmore Estate, Blue Ridge Parkway, and a deep network of vacation rentals, outdoor outfitters, and creative trades.
🏛️
The Piedmont
Charlotte · Raleigh · Durham · Greensboro · Winston-Salem
The economic engine of NC — Charlotte's banking hub, the Research Triangle's tech and biotech corridor, the Triad's manufacturing and logistics, and the state capital. Where most of the population lives and works.
🌊
The Coastal Plain
Wilmington · Outer Banks · Fayetteville · New Bern
Eastern NC anchored by Wilmington's port and film industry, the Outer Banks tourism economy, Fayetteville's military presence at Fort Liberty, and a growing healthcare and professional services base.
🪖
The Sandhills & Military Corridor
Fayetteville · Jacksonville · Southern Pines
Home to Fort Liberty (the largest US Army base) and Camp Lejeune (Marines), the military corridor drives a massive defense-adjacent small business economy — contractors, restaurants, healthcare, and professional services.

North Carolina's Economy & Business Climate

North Carolina is one of the most business-friendly states in America — with no inventory tax, a competitive corporate income tax rate, and a state government that has aggressively recruited global manufacturers for decades. The results speak for themselves: Toyota, Caterpillar, Corning, Siemens, and dozens of others have made NC home — alongside tech giants like Red Hat (IBM), Epic Games, and SAS.

North Carolina was one of the original 13 colonies, ratifying the U.S. Constitution in 1789. It has a deep agricultural heritage — tobacco shaped Eastern NC for generations, textiles and furniture built the Piedmont's economy — but today NC is defined by global banking (Charlotte), the Research Triangle's tech and biotech corridor, advanced manufacturing, military, healthcare, and tourism.

🛳️
Banking Capital — Charlotte
Charlotte is the second-largest banking center in the US, headquarters to Bank of America and Truist, with massive Wells Fargo operations — supporting a deep finance, legal, and professional services economy.
✈️
Research Triangle Park (RTP)
One of the largest research parks in the world — IBM, SAS, Cisco, GSK, and hundreds of biotech, pharma, and tech firms anchor the Raleigh-Durham-Chapel Hill corridor and drive a deep professional services ecosystem.
🏖️
Tourism — Outer Banks & Mountains
The Outer Banks (Nags Head, Cape Hatteras, Duck), Asheville and the Blue Ridge Parkway, and Wilmington's beaches collectively drive one of the South's largest tourism economies — supporting vacation rentals, restaurants, outfitters, and retail year-round.
🪖
Military — Fort Liberty & Camp Lejeune
Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg) near Fayetteville is the largest US Army base by population. Camp Lejeune (Jacksonville) anchors the Marines' East Coast operations. Together they drive defense contracting and a vast military-adjacent small business economy.
🏗️
Advanced Manufacturing — Piedmont Corridor
Toyota (Randolph County), Caterpillar (Sanford), Corning (Hickory), and hundreds of aerospace, auto-parts, and pharmaceutical manufacturers line the I-85 and I-40 corridors — driving a deep skilled-trades economy.

Key Interstate & Highway Infrastructure

North Carolina's road network is one of the most extensive in the Southeast — connecting the Mountains to the Outer Banks and positioning Charlotte and the Triad as major Southeast logistics hubs.

I-85 — Charlotte to the Triad
The diagonal spine through the Piedmont — connects Charlotte to Greensboro, Durham, and Virginia. The primary logistics artery for NC manufacturing.
I-77 — Charlotte's North-South Spine
Connects Charlotte north to Mooresville (Lowe's HQ) and Statesville, south to South Carolina. A primary commuter and logistics route through the largest NC metro.
I-95 — Eastern Coastal Plain
Runs north-south through eastern NC — Fayetteville, Rocky Mount, Lumberton — the major freight corridor between New York and Florida.
I-485 — Charlotte Beltway
The 67-mile loop around Charlotte connecting Concord, Pineville, Matthews, and the airport. Anchors Charlotte's suburban commercial growth.
I-40 — East-West Backbone
The longest interstate in NC — runs from Wilmington across the entire state through Raleigh, Greensboro, and Asheville. The state's primary east-west backbone.
I-26 — Asheville to Charleston
The southern interstate connecting Asheville and the NC mountains south to Spartanburg and Charleston. A scenic and freight route through the western Carolinas.

NC Landmarks & Cultural Identity

UNC Chapel Hill, NC State (Raleigh), Duke (Durham), Wake Forest (Winston-Salem), and 16 UNC-system schools collectively educate 250,000+ students — fueling NC's professional services, biotech, and healthcare workforces.
🌊
Outer Banks & Crystal Coast
200+ miles of barrier island coastline — Nags Head, Cape Hatteras, Duck, Ocracoke, and the Crystal Coast drive one of the East Coast's largest vacation-rental and tourism economies.
