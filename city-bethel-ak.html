Bethel's Business Scene

Bethel Is the Hub of
the Yukon-Kuskokwim
Delta.

Bethel is the regional center for roughly 50 villages across the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta — the healthcare, retail, air-freight, and government hub of a region the size of a small state. There is no road connection to anywhere else in Alaska: everything arrives by air, by river barge in summer, or over the Kuskokwim ice road in winter.

That geography creates opportunity — and real financial complexity. Bethel owners run on air freight and barge schedules, some of the highest costs in the country, a 6% city sales tax, and a mix of cash and subsistence economies. They need clean books and a partner who can keep up.

Bethel serves about 50 villages by air and river — making it the retail, medical, and freight center of the Y-K Delta, and one of Alaska's busiest airports.

📍 About Bethel, Alaska

The City Behind the Business.

Bethel is more than a zip code — it sits in the Bethel Census Area, a census area within Alaska's Unorganized Borough — there is no borough government here. Understanding its neighborhoods, roads, history, and economy is how we serve our clients better.

History & Economic Growth

Bethel sits on the traditional lands of the Yup'ik people, who have lived along the Kuskokwim for thousands of years. The modern town grew from a Moravian mission established in 1885 at the Yup'ik village of Mumtrekhlagamute.

Because the Kuskokwim is navigable this far upriver, Bethel became the transfer point between ocean barges and the villages — and later between jets and bush planes. Bethel Airport grew into one of the busiest in Alaska, and the Yukon-Kuskokwim Health Corporation, the Association of Village Council Presidents, and the Lower Kuskokwim School District made the town the administrative center of the Delta.

Today that hub role continues — with more small business owners than ever, and more who need clean books to grow with confidence.

Alaska's sales tax is entirely local and destination-based across 100+ jurisdictions (roughly 0% to 7.5%). Remote sellers and marketplace facilitators register once with the Alaska Remote Sellers Sales Tax Commission (ARSSTC) and file a single return covering the member jurisdictions. The economic nexus threshold is $100,000 in gross statewide sales; the 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025. We code your sales by destination so the filing is a report, not a research project.
