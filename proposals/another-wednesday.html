Where you're starting

A company built to own the gear, run by three people who’d rather be shooting

Another Wednesday exists for a specific reason: three freelancers went out on their own at the same time, bought a camera package together, and needed an entity that could own and insure the gear. Three years on, that’s still the shape of it — no office, no employees, one Chase checking and savings account, three cards, and a package now worth $35–40k.

The bookkeeping job landed on you on top of producing, and it’s the thing that keeps sliding. Categorization is current to roughly March; the receipts since — including the feature you ran through the company over the summer — are organized in folders but haven’t made it into QuickBooks. You catch up in bursts, a couple of months in a week, and then it slides again.

What you actually said you want is simpler than a full rebuild: a P&L honest enough to tell you whether to push harder on your rates or on the production company fee. That’s a real question and the books can answer it — they just aren’t set up to right now.

What we found

Four things worth fixing

01
The P&L can’t answer your question yet
The company profits exactly two ways — equipment rental and the production company fee. Right now both land in the same income line as the costs you rebill to clients, so there’s no way to see which one is carrying you. Splitting the income side is what turns “rates or fees?” into something you can read off a report each month.
02
2026 stops around March
Some accounts are categorized through March and nothing since has been entered or attached. Nine months of a calendar fiscal year is a lot to reconstruct in December, and it’s the reason the bank balance never quite tells you what you have.
03
Rebilled costs are sitting in cost of goods
Everything charged to a client went to COGS in 2025. That isn’t wrong, but it merges money you’re simply passing through with money Another Wednesday actually spends — so margin reads oddly and the two real profit lines stay buried.
04
All three partners draw from one account
Owner’s draws aren’t separated by partner, so there’s no running record of who has taken what. That record is the backbone of the ratio you work out at year end, and it has to be right before the 2027 S-corp restructure — it’s much easier to build now than to reconstruct later.

Scope of work

What I’ll do

  • One-time cleanup (January–September). Categorize and reconcile every Chase account and all three cards, attach the receipts you’ve been collecting, rebuild the chart of accounts around what a production company actually spends money on, split equipment rental and production fees into their own income lines, and separate each partner’s draws.
  • Ongoing every month. Keep it current as it happens — categorized, reconciled, receipts matched from the portal — and close each month with a P&L, balance sheet and cash summary. No more catching up three months in a week.
  • In step with your CPA. I own the monthly books; your CPA keeps the depreciation schedule and handles the returns and the S-corp work — now from numbers that don’t need reconstructing first.
