Best Accounting Software for Ecommerce Sellers (2026)

DCBy Daniel Cope·September 19, 2026·3 min read

For an online seller, the accounting software is only half the answer. The other half is the connector that gets Shopify, Amazon and other payouts into it correctly. I'm A2X certified and work in QuickBooks Online, so here's the setup I'd recommend at each stage, and where the alternatives fit.

Quick picks: most sellers → QuickBooks Online plus A2X. Want one entry per order instead of per payout → Webgility or Synder. Selling across borders with VAT → look hard at Link My Books. Just starting with a few sales a month → QuickBooks Online and a careful monthly summary entry.

Step one: the ledger

QuickBooks Online is where most US sellers should keep their books. Every CPA knows it, and it has the widest set of ecommerce connectors. Its Plus and Advanced plans add inventory tracking and classes, which help once you sell physical products across more than one channel. QuickBooks

Xero is the main alternative, with unlimited users and strong multi-currency support, and the same connectors work with it. Fewer US CPAs use it day to day, so ask yours first. My QuickBooks Online vs Xero comparison covers the trade-offs.

Step two: the connector (this is what most sellers are missing)

Shopify, Amazon and the other marketplaces pay you in batches, net of fees, refunds and adjustments. Book the deposit as sales and your revenue is understated and your fees vanish. A connector fixes that. The main difference between them is whether they post one summary per payout or every order.

Why I default to A2X

For most sellers, summary posting is the right call. Each payout becomes one entry that splits out sales, refunds, shipping, fees and taxes, and because it totals exactly what hit the bank, it matches the deposit in one click. Your P&L shows gross sales and real margins, and your ledger stays readable. A2X supports Shopify, Amazon, eBay, Etsy, Walmart, PayPal and more, and posts to QuickBooks Online and Xero. I explain how it works in What is A2X? A2X

When order-level detail is worth it

If you invoice wholesale customers on terms, need per-order profitability inside the ledger, or your accountant insists on it, an order-level connector like Webgility makes sense. Just know that thousands of orders a month means thousands of transactions to keep clean. Webgility

Setup mistakes that break ecommerce books

  • Everything in one sales account. Split gross sales, refunds, shipping income, platform fees and advertising.
  • Marketplace-collected sales tax in revenue. Amazon and other marketplaces collect and remit sales tax in many states. Map it so it doesn't inflate your sales. See sales tax nexus explained.
  • A start date that double-counts. Start the connector from the last reconciled date.
  • No clearing accounts. Shopify Payments, PayPal and each marketplace need their own, so each payout ties out.
  • Ignoring COGS. Without cost of goods sold, gross margin is fiction. Connectors like A2X can post COGS if you load product costs.

Ecommerce accounting FAQ

What is the best accounting software for ecommerce?

For most US sellers, QuickBooks Online paired with a connector like A2X. QuickBooks is what CPAs expect, and the connector turns Shopify and Amazon payouts into clean entries that match your bank deposits.

Do I need A2X if I use QuickBooks?

If you sell on Shopify, Amazon or other marketplaces with steady volume, usually yes. Without a connector, deposits get booked as sales, fees disappear, and the books don't reconcile.

Should my books show every order or one entry per payout?

For most sellers, one summarized entry per payout keeps the books readable and reconcilable. Order-level posting makes sense when you invoice wholesale customers or need per-order detail inside the ledger.

Is Xero or QuickBooks better for ecommerce?

Both work with the main connectors. QuickBooks Online is more common among US CPAs; Xero has unlimited users and strong multi-currency support. Ask your CPA which they prefer.

How do I handle marketplace sales tax in my books?

Where Amazon or another marketplace collects and remits sales tax for you, map it so it doesn't count as your revenue. Your connector setup should handle this; check it at setup.
