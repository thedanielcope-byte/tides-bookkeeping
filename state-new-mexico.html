📍 About New Mexico

Where Permian Oil Meets National-Lab Brainpower.

New Mexico's economy runs on energy first: the state's share of the Permian Basin makes it one of the top crude-oil producers in the country, and oil and gas revenue underwrites much of the state budget. Federal spending is the other pillar, anchored by Los Alamos and Sandia National Laboratories and major military installations. Tourism in the Land of Enchantment and a growing film and media production sector round out a small-population economy that punches above its weight.

The NM tax picture

New Mexico's signature quirk is that it does not levy a conventional sales tax at all. Instead it charges a Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) on the seller's total business receipts, with a statewide base rate around 5% and combined state, county, and municipal rates that typically run from roughly 5% up to more than 9% depending on where the sale happens (as of 2026 — confirm the current rate). The GRT applies to many services, not just goods, so service businesses that would owe nothing in other states can have real exposure here. New Mexico also has a personal income tax, graduated from about 1.5% up to a top rate near 5.9%, plus a flat corporate income tax around 5.9% (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). Getting GRT location codes and reporting right is where a lot of New Mexico small businesses trip up.
