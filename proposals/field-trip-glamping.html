Prepared for Field Trip Glamping Prepared by Dan Cope September 2026

Where you're starting

A healthy little operation that has outgrown in-house books.

Field Trip is a strong, well-run glampground — 23 units in Travelers Rest, running roughly $8,000 to $10,000 a month in operating expenses including labor. The business isn't the problem. The bookkeeping has simply been handled in-house, and it has quietly outgrown that.

Pamela Lenci at PJL Hospitality has already done the hard part: a full audit of the operation and a rebuilt, USALI-aligned chart of accounts, structured and numbered and ready to go. What's left is implementing it, bringing the history onto it, and moving the reporting to accrual so the numbers actually reflect how the business performed — not just when cash moved.

The books have been kept in QuickBooks Online and are in reasonable shape. This is a rebuild, not a rescue.

What needs doing

Four things between here and books you can plan from.

01
The new chart of accounts isn’t live yet
Pamela’s USALI-aligned structure is built and ready. It needs to be rolled into QuickBooks and applied backward through the history so year-over-year comparisons actually line up.
02
Fourteen months of history to verify and reconcile
Every bank and card transaction from August 1, 2025 through go-live gets reconciled to statements, recoded to the new accounts, and tied out — one operating account and two to three credit cards.
03
Revenue is grouped by channel, not by unit type
Today it’s an Airbnb total and a Hipcamp total. Restructured by unit type, you can finally see which domes, tents, and cabins actually earn — and carry that mapping into the new property-management system.
04
Cash-basis reporting on a business that books months ahead
Guests and event parties pay long before they arrive. On accrual, that revenue lands in the period it’s earned, deferred revenue shows on the balance sheet, and the P&L stops swinging with deposit timing.

Scope of work

Roll it out, rebuild the history, then keep it clean.

  • One-time rebuild & catch-up (August 2025 – September 2026). Implement the new chart of accounts in QuickBooks, reconcile every bank and card account to statements, recode the history onto the new structure, restructure revenue by unit type, convert reporting to accrual, and give the balance sheet a full pass — until the books are current, balanced, and defensible.
  • Ongoing every month. Categorize and reconcile as it happens, track deferred revenue on advance bookings, keep sales and accommodations tax straight, and close each month on accrual with a P&L and balance sheet you can hand to anyone. Plus a monthly review call.
  • In step with your CPA. I own the monthly books; your CPA handles the filings — now working from clean numbers on a chart of accounts that makes sense to them.
