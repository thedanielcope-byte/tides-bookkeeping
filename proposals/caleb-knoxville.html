Bookkeeping Proposal

Seven months to catch up, and a number on every house

Prepared for Caleb — house flipping, Knoxville TN Prepared by Dan Cope · Tides Bookkeeping October 14, 2026

Where you’re starting

The business works. The books stopped in April.

Two years of flipping houses in Knoxville, a couple of them in the air at once. QuickBooks is already there, with one bank account and one credit card running through it, and a set of Google Sheets alongside it.

Bookkeeping has been open since April. Seven months of a single account and a single card is a short catch-up, and the smaller it stays the cheaper it stays — which is why now is the right time to close it out rather than at tax time.

The part worth more than the catch-up is what comes after. Flips don’t tell you anything from one profit and loss for the year. They tell you something house by house: what you paid, what the rehab actually ran against what you thought it would, what the holding cost while it sat, and what was left at closing. That’s what I’d build you.

How I’d set it up

Four pieces

01
Every house becomes its own project in QuickBooks
Purchase, rehab, materials, subs, interest, insurance and utilities all land on the house they belong to. When it closes, you see what that one flip actually made — not a single pile of “job expenses” for the year.
02
April through October gets entered and reconciled
One bank account and one credit card, seven months. That is a small enough file to bring current quickly, and every month gets tied to the statement rather than eyeballed.
03
The spreadsheets stop being the books
Your Google Sheets are useful history, and I’ll use them to name the houses, sort out what belongs to which, and fill gaps the bank can’t explain. After that QuickBooks is the record and the sheets are the backup.
04
What goes into a house stays with the house until it sells
Purchase price and rehab are capitalized while you hold it, not written off as you spend. The profit shows up at closing, in the month you actually earned it, which is the version your CPA needs and the one that tells you whether to buy the next one.

Getting started

Four things to start

  1. Say yes and the agreement follows. Month-to-month, no long-term contract. Payment runs through Stripe: the $900 catch-up once on signing, then $299 monthly from November 1.
  2. Invite me as your accountant. In QuickBooks, add dan@tidesbookkeeping.com under Manage users. Leave the bank account and the credit card connected until the seven months are reconciled.
  3. Send what you’ve been keeping. The Google Sheets, and the closing statement for anything you bought or sold since April. Addresses are enough to start — I’ll build the project list from them.
  4. Fill in two blanks. How the business is set up and who else is on it, and what’s financing the houses — hard money, a line of credit, your own cash. Both change how the books are built, so I’d rather have them on day one than guess.
