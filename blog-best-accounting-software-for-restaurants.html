Best Accounting Software for Restaurants (2026)

DCBy Daniel Cope·September 19, 2026·6 min read

I keep books for restaurants inside QuickBooks Online and Restaurant365 every month, so this isn't a list pulled from vendor websites. The short answer: QuickBooks Online is the right call for most independent restaurants with one or two locations, usually paired with your POS's sales sync. Restaurant365 earns its higher price once you're running several locations or want food cost, prime cost and labor in one system. Here's the full picture.

Quick picks: most independents → QuickBooks Online. Three or more locations, or you want inventory and daily P&L built in → Restaurant365. On QuickBooks or Xero but want invoice automation and food cost → add MarginEdge (any POS) or xtraCHEF (Toast). Food truck or pop-up → start simple with QuickBooks Simple Start or Wave.

1. QuickBooks Online: best for most independent restaurants

QuickBooks Online is the default for independent restaurants, and for good reason. Every CPA knows it, every lender asks for its reports, and every major POS can send it sales data. Toast, for example, can post a daily sales journal entry into QuickBooks through its xtraCHEF sync, so sales, tips, sales tax and card fees land where they belong without anyone typing them in.

What it doesn't do is think like a restaurant. There's no recipe costing, no food cost percentage report and no prime cost dashboard. You get those numbers by building the chart of accounts right (food, beer, liquor and wine as separate cost of goods sold accounts; front- and back-of-house labor split) and running the math each month. Plus and Advanced plans add class and location tracking, which matters once you have a second concept or location.

Pick it if: you have one or two locations, a straightforward menu, and you want the software your CPA already uses. More on the trade-offs in Is QuickBooks good for restaurants? QuickBooks site

2. Restaurant365: best for multi-location and data-driven operators

Restaurant365 is built for restaurants from the ground up. It combines the general ledger with inventory, recipe costing, scheduling, payroll and accounts payable automation. Your POS uploads sales every night, and they flow into accounting, inventory and labor reporting without manual entry. That's how you get a daily P&L and prime cost by location instead of waiting for month-end.

The catch is cost and complexity. It's priced per location and costs several times what QuickBooks does, and it takes real setup to get the value out of it. I work inside clients' Restaurant365 instances every month; when it's set up well it's the best tool in this list, and when it isn't, owners pay for features nobody uses. We offer Restaurant365 bookkeeping for exactly that reason.

Pick it if: you run three or more locations, you're opening another soon, or you want food cost, labor and inventory counts in the same system as your books. Restaurant365 site

3. Xero: the main QuickBooks alternative

Xero covers the same general-accounting ground as QuickBooks and includes unlimited users on every plan, which helps when a partner, a manager and your bookkeeper all need access. Like QuickBooks, it has no restaurant-specific tools of its own and leans on POS integrations and add-ons. In the US, payroll runs through a partner such as Gusto. The bigger practical issue is that fewer US CPAs work in Xero day to day, so ask yours before switching. My QuickBooks Online vs Xero comparison goes deeper. Xero site

4. MarginEdge: food cost control without switching systems

MarginEdge isn't accounting software. It's a back-office layer that sits in front of it. Your team photographs invoices or forwards vendor PDFs, MarginEdge processes them (their help center says within 24–48 hours), and approved invoices flow into your accounting system as bills along with nightly sales journal entries. It connects to QuickBooks, Xero and Sage Intacct, and it can also feed Restaurant365.

Pick it if: you want invoice-level food cost and recipe costing but aren't ready for Restaurant365's price or complexity. MarginEdge site

5. xtraCHEF by Toast: the Toast restaurant's add-on

If you're on Toast, xtraCHEF is Toast's own back-office tool. It digitizes vendor invoices down to the line item and can send daily sales journal entries into QuickBooks Online automatically. Toast Payroll also has its own QuickBooks Online sync as of 2026. For a Toast restaurant on QuickBooks, this is often the simplest way to close the gap on invoices and daily sales. Toast + QuickBooks integration

6. Sage Intacct and 7. Wave: the two ends of the range

Sage Intacct is for restaurant groups with multiple entities and an in-house finance team that need consolidated reporting. It's more than an independent restaurant needs, and it's outside what we set up. Sage Intacct

Wave has a free starting tier that works for a food truck, pop-up or tiny catering business with simple sales. Once you have employees, a second location or a POS doing real volume, move to QuickBooks. Wave

How to choose

  1. Count your locations. One or two usually means QuickBooks Online. Three or more is where Restaurant365 starts paying for itself.
  2. Check your POS. Make sure it can post daily sales to the software you pick. Toast, Square and Clover all connect to QuickBooks; Restaurant365 integrates with most major POS systems. Comparing POS systems too? See Toast vs Square vs Clover.
  3. Decide how much you care about food cost. If invoice-level food cost matters and you're on QuickBooks or Xero, budget for MarginEdge or xtraCHEF.
  4. Ask your CPA. They'll be working with whatever you choose at tax time.
  5. Price the whole stack. Accounting software plus add-ons plus payroll, per location, per month.

What matters more than the software

Whichever software you pick, these are the mistakes that actually wreck restaurant books:

  • Booking bank deposits as sales. Card deposits are net of fees, tips and refunds. Record daily sales from the POS, then match the deposits.
  • Treating tips as income. Tips belong to staff, so they're a liability until paid out. The IRS covers tip reporting in Publication 531.
  • Sales tax in revenue. Sales tax you collect is owed to the state. It's a liability, not sales.
  • Delivery apps booked net. Record DoorDash and Uber Eats sales gross and the commissions as an expense, or your sales and margins will both be wrong.
  • No prime cost tracking. Food, beverage and labor are most of your costs. Track them every month with our free prime cost calculatorprime cost calculatorprime cost calculator.

Restaurant accounting software FAQ

What is the best accounting software for a small restaurant?

For most independent restaurants with one or two locations, QuickBooks Online. Every CPA knows it, and major POS systems like Toast, Square and Clover can post daily sales into it. Add MarginEdge or xtraCHEF if you want invoice-level food cost.

Is Restaurant365 worth the cost?

Usually once you have three or more locations, or when you want daily P&L, prime cost, inventory and scheduling in one system. It's priced per location and costs several times what QuickBooks does, so for a single simple location it's often more than you need. See Restaurant365 vs QuickBooks.

Can QuickBooks track restaurant food cost?

Not at the recipe level. You can set up cost of goods sold accounts by category (food, beer, liquor, wine) and calculate food cost percentage each month. For invoice-level and recipe costing, add MarginEdge or xtraCHEF, or use Restaurant365.

What's the difference between MarginEdge and Restaurant365?

MarginEdge is a back-office add-on that processes invoices and tracks food cost, then sends the data into your accounting software. Restaurant365 is the accounting software itself, with the back office built in.

Do I still need a bookkeeper with restaurant software?

Yes. The software records transactions; someone still has to reconcile every account, book tips and sales tax correctly, handle delivery-app payouts, close the month and hand your CPA a clean file.
