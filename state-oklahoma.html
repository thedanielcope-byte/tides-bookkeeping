📍 About Oklahoma

Where Oil Country Meets Aerospace Alley.

Oklahoma's economy is anchored by energy — oil and natural gas remain the dominant industry, with a growing wind-power sector alongside it. Aerospace and aviation is one of the state's fastest-growing sectors, with hundreds of companies clustered around Oklahoma City and Tinker Air Force Base. Agriculture, especially cattle and wheat, rounds out a diverse economy spread between the Oklahoma City and Tulsa metros.

The OK tax picture

Oklahoma has been steadily cutting taxes. Effective for tax year 2026, the state consolidated its personal income tax into three brackets and lowered the top rate to about 4.5% (down from 4.75%), with revenue-triggered cuts pointing toward further reductions (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). The state sales tax rate is 4.5%, but Oklahoma allows heavy local add-ons, so combined city-and-county rates commonly reach 9% or more (as of 2026 — confirm current rate). Corporations pay a flat 4% corporate income tax, and oil and gas producers pay a gross production (severance) tax of roughly 7% on most wells. For most small businesses, the headline is a shrinking income tax paired with relatively high local sales tax.
