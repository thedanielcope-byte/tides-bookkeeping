Bookkeeping Proposal

Books for Bushel & Bee, with the same bill every month and no surprises.

Prepared for Kayla Applebay Prepared by Dan Cope September 29, 2026

Where you’re starting

Books in good shape. A bookkeeping firm that isn’t.

Bushel & Bee is Leavenworth’s cider taproom and bottle shop, with a team of 5 to 7 and sales running $35,000 to $75,000 a month depending on the season, with Oktoberfest and the Christmas lights at the top. You’re on QuickBooks Online, two Square accounts, Fintech for most of your distributors, Faire for snacks and merchandise, and ADP for payroll.

Your books are current and your chart of accounts works, so there’s nothing to rebuild. What you need is a bookkeeper who bills the same amount every month, gets your investors their K-1s on time, and keeps an eye on inventory. The plan below takes over with a light setup and has you on a clean monthly routine starting October 1.

What we’ll build in

Four things that make a taproom’s books useful.

01
Every purchase to the right place
About 85 to 90% of your distributors are on Fintech, so I pull the reports straight from Fintech and split each payment by distributor and category. Faire orders and the checks your staff photograph with their invoices get the same treatment, with fewer questions coming back to you.
02
Inventory and keg deposits, every month
Your keg spreadsheet already tracks what goes in and out. In the books, keg deposits are tracked as deposits, not beer cost. Your month-end Square inventory report sets cost of goods by category, so you can see how fast cider, mead, beer and non-alcoholic each turn.
03
Two Square accounts, one clean set of books
Taproom sales and your events and merchandise account each post as daily summaries, not every single transaction, so there’s never a duplicate mess to clean up. Deposits into your POS account are matched, with card fees, sales tax and tips split out.
04
Investors paid on the waterfall, K-1s on time
Every distribution is recorded against your waterfall agreement, with a running balance toward each investor’s original investment. With the books closed every month, your CPA gets a year-end package in January and your investors aren’t waiting until the deadline for their K-1s.

Scope of work

A quick handover, then books closed every month.

  • Setup (one-time). Access to QuickBooks, your bank, both Square accounts, Fintech, Faire and ADP; distributors mapped to categories; bank rules built; payroll set up to post correctly; your starting inventory and keg deposit balances in place; and a review of your most recent closed month. If that review turns up anything that needs fixing, I’ll quote it separately before doing any work.
  • Monthly bookkeeping, closed every month. Square deposits matched and split, Fintech, Faire and check purchases categorized, both checking accounts and savings reconciled, payroll recorded, your Washington excise tax return filed, and the month closed with a P&L, balance sheet and inventory by category.
  • Ready for your CPA. I don’t do tax returns; I’m the monthly engine that feeds your CPA. Whoever you choose gets reconciled books, investor distributions and a year-end package in January, and I’m glad to talk with any CPA you’re considering so the handoff is easy.
