What we found

Four things worth fixing

01
Tradition’s 2026 hasn’t been entered
Ten months across two checking accounts and three credit cards, plus the monthly seller-note and HELOC payments. It picks up exactly where the 2025 year-end leaves off, so the two years tie out.
02
Poppy is close, but not closed
Some accounts are current and others stop in January. Until every account is reconciled through the same date, the P&L looks finished without being finished — and that’s the report a budget gets built on.
03
Payroll arrives as lump withdrawals at both stores
Gusto runs payroll for about 16 people, but it isn’t connected to QuickBooks. Connecting it posts wages and payroll taxes properly per store — and makes it easy to hand payroll to your manager when you’re ready.
04
Margins depend on Shopify costs you don’t fully trust
With hundreds of vendors and product costs entered by more than one person, cost of goods is only as good as the last entry. Tightening how Shopify sales, fees and costs reach QuickBooks is what makes each store’s margin worth reading.
