What we found

Four things to get right while it’s being built.

01
There’s no accounting system yet
Everything from 2026 lives in Wells Fargo and your card statements. QuickBooks Online Simple Start is all you need — the chart of accounts gets built around what a brokerage actually has: commission income, agent splits, desk and transaction fees, and your marketing and systems spend. Built once, correctly, so it still works when you’re running it yourself and when you’re at fifteen agents.
02
You’re an S-corp and haven’t taken a salary this year
Your CPA moved you to an S-corp, which means reasonable compensation has to be on the books before December 31. He sets the number; I get payroll running and record it. This is the easiest thing to miss when you’re doing your own books, and the hardest to fix once the year closes.
03
SkySlope handles the deal. It doesn’t handle the books.
SkySlope tracks the transaction, the disbursement and the splits, and it’s good at that. Your books still live in QuickBooks either way. You were quoted an accounting migration on top of it — you don’t need it, and you won’t need it once QuickBooks is set up properly.
04
Steve’s cap isn’t being tracked anywhere
He pays a $400 monthly desk fee plus a transaction fee that stops once he hits his cap, and nothing keeps a running total today. That gets built now, with a saved report you can run in one click — so it keeps counting whether I’m in the file or you are, and it holds up as you recruit.
