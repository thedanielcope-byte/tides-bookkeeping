Where you're starting

Two growing stores, one hallway apart — and one of them needs its first set of books

You’ve run Poppy – Home & Gift for six years, and its books are in QuickBooks and largely current. In July 2025 you bought Tradition Children’s Market, and it’s having a great year — on pace for roughly $650k, with Poppy around $380k. Together you’re closing in on the $1M mark.

Tradition’s QuickBooks file was set up this week, so its July–December 2025 books start from zero — and your CPA needs them before October 1 to file on the extension. That’s the first job, and it’s the only thing this agreement covers.

After that, the goal is the one you named: a budget you can hold to, a clear read on what each store actually earns, and the costs that should go — so the decisions stop running on feel, and paying yourself stays sustainable.

What we found

Four things worth fixing

01
Tradition’s 2025 starts from a blank file
Six months of a newly purchased store — two checking accounts and three credit cards — need to be categorized and reconciled against statements, with opening balances set from the purchase date, on a one-week clock.
02
The purchase itself has to be recorded right
The monthly payment to the previous owner and the HELOC-funded down payment aren’t ordinary expenses. Recorded properly, the note, your capital in, and what you take back out each stay visible — and your CPA sees the acquisition the way it actually happened.
03
Payroll lands as lump bank withdrawals
Gusto runs payroll for about 16 people across both stores, but it isn’t connected to QuickBooks, so wages and payroll taxes are pieced together from bank lines. Connecting it splits the cost properly by store — and makes it easy to hand payroll to your manager later.
04
Margins depend on Shopify costs you don’t fully trust
With hundreds of vendors and product costs entered by more than one person, cost of goods is only as good as the last entry. Tightening how sales and costs flow from Shopify into QuickBooks is what makes a per-store margin — and a budget — worth reading.
