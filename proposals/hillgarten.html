Where you’re starting

A real business running on statements instead of books.

Hillgarten is doing serious volume — roughly $600,000 a year through the checking account, with a season that runs hard from March through October. The operation works. What’s missing is the accounting behind it: there is no bookkeeping system in place, so 2025 and 2026 exist only as bank and card statements in a folder.

That’s a bigger deal than it sounds, because the numbers are already telling you something. Sales are holding steady year over year, but the cash isn’t — and in June 2026, $109,391 came in and $108,237 went out. A month that busy should not finish that thin. Without books, there’s no way to see which costs are doing it.

None of this is unusual for a seasonal bar, and none of it is hard to fix. It just has to be built once, properly, and then kept up.

What we found

Four things standing between you and books you can trust.

01
There are no books to work from
Nothing has been kept in an accounting system, so 2025 and 2026 have to be rebuilt from the statements themselves — roughly 4,500 transactions across three accounts.
02
Sales are flat, but the cash isn’t
January through August, money into checking was $460,639 in 2025 and $445,401 in 2026 — essentially even. Yet the account went from $104,222 last September to $16,598 this May. Clean books tell you where that went.
03
Square deposits arrive already netted
Every payout lands as one lump sum with gross sales, sales tax, tips, and processing fees folded together. Until those are broken out, your true sales and your real card costs are both invisible.
04
Business and personal share one card
The AMEX carries genuine costs — food distribution, hardware, waste hauling — alongside clearly personal charges. Roughly 1,300 lines need sorting so your P&L shows the bar, not your life.

Scope of work

Build it right once, then keep it that way.

  • One-time cleanup (January 2025 – September 2026). Build the chart of accounts in Puzzle, then reconstruct and reconcile all three accounts month by month — Square sales broken into gross sales, tax, tips and fees; payroll recorded properly; business separated from personal — until 2025 and 2026 are current, balanced, and closed.
  • Ongoing every month. Your accounts feed straight into Puzzle, so I’m watching the transactions as they land rather than opening a shoebox in January. Categorize and reconcile as it happens, keep cost-of-goods and payroll on the books, and close each month with a P&L and balance sheet you can actually run the bar from — and see for yourself any day in between.
  • One entity, clearly bounded. This covers Smith Granville Enterprises, LLC (dba Hillgarten). Rent or transfers involving 412 Devon Main LLC and Jane Lex Properties are recorded as they hit the operating account; separate books for those entities can be added any time.
