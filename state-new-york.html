📍 About New York

Home of Wall Street.

New York has one of the largest economies of any U.S. state, with a gross domestic product north of $2 trillion — bigger than most countries. Finance and insurance, centered on Wall Street and the New York City securities industry, is the single largest sector, followed by professional and business services, a fast-growing technology industry, and tourism. Healthcare, education, and media round out the downstate economy, while upstate adds meaningful manufacturing and agriculture.

The NY tax picture

New York is a high-tax state, and its structure has an unusual wrinkle for city businesses. The personal income tax is steeply graduated, running from roughly 3.9% up to a top rate of 10.9% on the highest earners (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). On top of that, New York City levies its own separate resident income tax of about 3.078% to 3.876%, so New York City residents pay both state and city income tax — a combination most states don't have. State sales tax has a 4% base, but local add-ons push combined rates up to 8.875% in New York City (as of 2026 — confirm current rate). Businesses organized as corporations also face the state Corporate Franchise Tax, generally 6.5% on the business income base (7.25% above $5 million), so entity choice and clean books matter a great deal here.
