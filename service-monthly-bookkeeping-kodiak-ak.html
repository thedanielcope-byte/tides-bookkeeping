Monthly Bookkeeping for
Kodiak Businesses.

Clean monthly books are what keep a seasonal economy profitable. Here is how monthly bookkeeping fits the businesses driving the Kodiak economy, inside the Kodiak Island Borough.

Kodiak, in the Kodiak Island Borough, is one of the largest commercial fishing ports in the United States. The fleet, the processors, and the marine-service firms that keep them running carry books full of permits and quota, gear, fuel, vessel maintenance, crew shares, and fish-ticket deliveries — a structure that looks nothing like a Lower 48 retail ledger.

Coast Guard Base Kodiak, the largest USCG base in the country, adds a steady federal payroll and a base of supporting vendors: contractors, housing, food service, logistics, and professional firms. Those businesses need books that handle contract revenue, multiple accounts, and real payroll complexity without falling behind.

And it is an island. Everything arrives by barge or plane, so freight is a permanent, material line item — and one of the easiest ways to misread cost of goods if it is dumped in a catch-all account. Tides delivers your reconciliations, profit and loss, and balance sheet every month, fully remote. Want the full local picture? See our Kodiak bookkeeping services page.

The Alaska Tax Reality

Alaska Sales Tax,
Handled Monthly.

No state sales tax. No state income tax. But more than 100 boroughs and cities levy their own sales tax, and where you sell decides what you owe. Here is what that means for a Kodiak business.

Alaska levies no statewide sales tax, and the Kodiak Island Borough does not impose a general borough-wide sales tax — but the City of Kodiak does, at a rate reported around 7 percent (as of 2026 — confirm the current rate with the City). That is one of the higher municipal rates in Alaska, and it makes the in-city versus out-of-city question a genuine bookkeeping issue rather than a technicality. We track taxable versus exempt sales as they happen and reconcile the liability every month. Sell remotely into taxing jurisdictions past $100,000 in gross statewide sales and you file through ARSSTC — the 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025.

Rates and thresholds above are current to the best of our knowledge as of 2026 and are provided for general information only. Local ordinances change — confirm the current rate with your borough or city before you rely on it. Tides provides bookkeeping, not tax advice or tax filing.
