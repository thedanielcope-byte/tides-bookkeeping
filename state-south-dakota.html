📍 About South Dakota

No Income Tax — On People or Companies.

South Dakota's economy starts with agriculture — corn, soybeans, wheat, and cattle ranching are the state's number-one industry. Sioux Falls became a national credit-card and banking hub after the state removed its interest-rate cap in 1980, drawing Citibank and later Wells Fargo, First Premier, and a major trust-and-wealth industry. Tourism is a third pillar, built around Mount Rushmore, the Black Hills, the Badlands, and the Sturgis rally, with Sanford Health and Avera Health among the largest employers.

The SD tax picture

South Dakota's signature fact is simple and rare: there is no personal income tax and no corporate income tax — your paycheck and your business profits aren't taxed at the state level at all. The state funds itself largely through sales tax. The state sales tax rate is 4.2% as of 2026, a temporary reduction that is scheduled to return to 4.5% on July 1, 2027 (confirm the current rate before filing). Municipalities may add a general municipal sales tax of up to 2%, plus in some cases a separate municipal gross receipts tax on things like lodging and prepared food, so combined rates commonly land around 6%. With no income tax to file, South Dakota is one of the most business-friendly tax environments in the country — the focus is on getting sales tax right.
