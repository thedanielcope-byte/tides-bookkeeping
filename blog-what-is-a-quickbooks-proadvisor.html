What Is a QuickBooks ProAdvisor? (And How to Verify One)

DCBy Daniel Cope·September 18, 2026·4 min read

A QuickBooks ProAdvisor is an accountant or bookkeeper who has joined Intuit's ProAdvisor program. A certified ProAdvisor has also passed one of Intuit's certification exams. Those are two different things, and the second one has an expiration date. Here's what the title means, what the exams cover, and how to check anyone's status in about a minute.

ProAdvisor vs. certified ProAdvisor

Joining the ProAdvisor program is free for accounting professionals who use QuickBooks Online Accountant. There's no test to join. That makes someone a ProAdvisor, and it gets them a listing in Intuit's Find a ProAdvisor directory.

Certification is the part with a test. Intuit's ProAdvisor Academy runs training tracks that end in an exam, and the certifications that currently count include:

  • QuickBooks Online Level 1 covers the core: setting up a company, the chart of accounts, recording sales and expenses, banking and standard reports.
  • QuickBooks Online Level 2 goes deeper. Intuit's directory labels people who hold it "Advanced ProAdvisor."
  • QuickBooks Workforce is the payroll certification. It was called QuickBooks Payroll until Intuit renamed its payroll products in 2026.
  • Intuit Enterprise Suite covers Intuit's product for larger, multi-entity businesses.

One more thing every listing says, and it's worth knowing: ProAdvisors are not employed by Intuit. They're independent firms Intuit has trained and tested.

What the exams actually cover

I've gone through the Level 1, Level 2 and payroll tracks, and they're more practical than people expect. Level 2 is a good example. It covers:

  • Tracking profitability by sub-customer and by project, including progress invoicing.
  • Price rules, customer and vendor prepayments, and delayed charges and credits.
  • Billable expenses and billable time, with markups.
  • Corporate credit cards, failed payments, voided checks and bank-feed matching problems.
  • Year-end cleanup: stuck undeposited funds, writing off invoices, journal entries, and filing and correcting 1099s.
  • Budgets, plus custom reporting with tags, custom fields, classes and locations.

The payroll track covers payroll tax setup, wage types and gross-to-net pay, deductions and benefits, time off, running scheduled and final paychecks, depositing and filing payroll taxes, reconciling payroll, and W-2s.

That's a useful baseline. Someone who has passed Level 2 has been tested on the features that tend to get misused in small-business files, like undeposited funds, prepayments and classes.

Certifications have to be renewed every year

This is the part most business owners don't know. Intuit requires certified ProAdvisors to recertify every year. In 2026 the window ran from April 28 to June 30. Pass the recertification exam and the certification carries forward. Miss it and it comes off the directory.

So a badge on a website, including ours, is a claim. The directory is the proof, because it only shows what's current.

ProAdvisor tiers aren't certifications

You'll also see Silver, Gold, Platinum and Elite. Those are tiers in the ProAdvisor program, and they're based on points. A firm earns points for the QuickBooks and payroll subscriptions it manages for clients and for active certifications, and points are pooled across everyone in the firm.

A tier tells you roughly how much QuickBooks business a firm handles. It doesn't tell you how good the work is. A two-person firm doing careful work can sit at a lower tier than a large firm doing volume.

How to verify a ProAdvisor in 60 seconds

  1. Go to Intuit's Find a ProAdvisor directory at proadvisor.intuit.com.
  2. Search the person's name or their firm's name.
  3. Open their profile and look at "Certified expert in." That section lists current certifications only.
  4. Check the reviews on the listing. They come from clients through Intuit, not from the firm's own website.
  5. Ask them which certification they hold and when they last recertified. A good bookkeeper will answer without blinking.

What a certification won't tell you

A certification says someone knows the software. It doesn't tell you everything you need to know about hiring them:

  • It's not a CPA license. ProAdvisors can be bookkeepers, accountants or CPAs. If you need tax returns prepared or tax advice, you want a CPA or enrolled agent. Here's how bookkeepers, CPAs and accountants differ.
  • It doesn't show industry experience. Restaurants, contractors and ecommerce sellers each have their own traps. Ask what share of their clients look like you.
  • It doesn't show how they work. Ask who actually touches your books, what date the month is closed by, whether they reconcile every account monthly, and whether pricing is flat or hourly.

If you're in the middle of that decision, how to find a bookkeeper and what a bookkeeper costs cover the rest.

QuickBooks ProAdvisor FAQ

What is a QuickBooks ProAdvisor?

An accountant or bookkeeper who has joined Intuit's ProAdvisor program. A certified ProAdvisor has also passed at least one Intuit certification exam, such as QuickBooks Online Level 1, Level 2 or QuickBooks Workforce (payroll).

Is a QuickBooks ProAdvisor a CPA?

Not necessarily. ProAdvisor is an Intuit program for accounting professionals of all kinds. Some ProAdvisors are CPAs, many are bookkeepers. A CPA is a state license; a ProAdvisor certification is software training and testing.

How long does ProAdvisor certification last?

One year. Intuit requires certified ProAdvisors to recertify annually, during a window that ran April 28 to June 30 in 2026. Certifications that aren't renewed drop off the directory.

What is an Advanced ProAdvisor?

Intuit's directory label for a ProAdvisor who holds the QuickBooks Online Level 2 certification.

How do I check if a ProAdvisor is certified?

Search their name at Intuit's Find a ProAdvisor directory and look at the "Certified expert in" section on their profile. It shows current certifications only.
