📍 About New Ellenton, SC

The Town Behind the Business.

New Ellenton is a purpose-built town with a singular story — created from scratch in the early 1950s when the federal government needed a new home for families displaced by the Savannah River Site. Understanding that history, the SC-19 corridor, and the SRS workforce is how we serve our clients better.

History & Economic Growth

New Ellenton was created in 1951–52 as a relocation town. When the federal government chose the land south of Aiken to build the Savannah River Site (SRS) — a nuclear weapons material production facility — the existing communities of old Ellenton, Dunbarton, Meyers Mill, and several others were absorbed into the SRS property. Residents and businesses were relocated, and "New Ellenton" was platted just north of the SRS boundary along SC-19 specifically to house them.

From day one, New Ellenton has been a commuter community for SRS workers. SC-19 — known locally as Atomic Road — runs straight from the SRS gates north through town toward Aiken, and the daily rhythm of the town still follows shift changes at the site. Today, SRS and the adjacent Savannah River National Laboratory employ thousands of federal workers, scientists, engineers, and contractors from companies like Savannah River Nuclear Solutions, Centerra, and Battelle.

That federal workforce supports a tight cluster of local small businesses — trades, restaurants, service shops, and contractors — many of them family-run for generations. They need clean books to grow with confidence.
