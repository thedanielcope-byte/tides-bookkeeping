About the Area

Dougherty County Is
Southwest Georgia's
Regional Hub.

Dougherty County is the commercial, medical, and cultural center of Southwest Georgia, anchored by Albany -- the largest city in the region and the hub a dozen surrounding rural counties depend on for healthcare, retail, and professional services. The county sits on the Flint River, where US-19, US-82, and GA-300 converge.

Phoebe Putney Health System is the dominant employer, Marine Corps Logistics Base Albany drives steady defense and logistics spending, and Procter & Gamble anchors manufacturing. Around all of it sits some of the most productive farmland in the state -- peanuts, pecans, and cotton -- feeding an agribusiness economy that reaches far beyond the county line.

History & Economic Identity

Dougherty County was created in 1853 and Albany, founded in 1836 on a bend of the Flint River, became its seat. The river made Albany a shipping point for cotton long before the railroads arrived, and the county grew into the trade center for a wide swath of Southwest Georgia farmland.

Phoebe Putney Health System is the county's dominant employer today -- a regional referral hospital that draws patients from across Southwest Georgia and anchors a large ecosystem of private practices, specialists, imaging centers, and home health agencies. Marine Corps Logistics Base Albany, one of the Marine Corps' primary maintenance and supply installations, adds steady federal spending and a long tail of defense contractors and logistics firms.

Manufacturing rounds out the base, with the Procter & Gamble plant among the county's largest industrial employers. And agriculture never left: peanuts, pecans, and cotton from the surrounding counties move through Albany, supporting equipment dealers, processors, trucking outfits, and farm service businesses that all need books kept the way a seasonal business actually earns.
