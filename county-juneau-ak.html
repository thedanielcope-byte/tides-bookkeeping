About the Area

The Capital City -
Reachable Only by
Air or Water.

The City and Borough of Juneau is a unified city-borough: the city and the borough are one and the same government, so there is a single assembly and a single set of local filings. It is also the only U.S. state capital with no road connection to the rest of its state - you arrive by plane or by ferry, and everything you sell arrives the same way.

That geography defines the economy. State government is the largest employer, and the legislative session from January into May brings a wave of legislators, staff, and lobbyists into town every winter. Then in May the cruise ships arrive and the summer visitor economy takes over. Between the two, Juneau runs a double season that most towns never experience.

Underneath both sits a durable base: commercial fishing, two hardrock mines, the Coast Guard, and the professional services firms that serve a capital. It is a small market with an unusually sophisticated set of businesses - and unusually complicated cash flow.

History & Economic Identity

Juneau was born from the 1880 gold strike that Joe Juneau and Richard Harris made in the gulch above what is now downtown. The Alaska-Juneau and Treadwell mines made it the biggest city in the territory, and in 1906 the capital moved here from Sitka. The mines closed during World War II, and government replaced gold as the town's economic engine.

The single strangest fact about Juneau's economy is that you cannot drive here. Glacier Highway runs about forty miles and dead-ends. Everything - inventory, equipment, building materials, produce - arrives by barge or plane. Freight cost is not a rounding error on a Juneau P&L; it is a structural cost line that has to be tracked deliberately, and it is the reason margin math that works in Seattle does not work here.

The other defining feature is the double season. From January into May the legislature is in session and the town fills with legislators, staff, lobbyists, and press - filling hotels and restaurants in what would otherwise be the dead of winter. Then the session ends, and within weeks the cruise ships start arriving, bringing well over a million passengers across a summer. Two peaks, two very different customer bases, and a genuinely quiet shoulder in between.

🏛️
State of Alaska - Largest Employer
Juneau is the seat of state government, and the State of Alaska is far and away the biggest employer in the borough. The January-to-May legislative session is a real, recurring revenue event for hotels, restaurants, and short-term rentals.
🚢
Cruise Tourism
More than a million cruise passengers a season come ashore downtown between roughly May and September. Whale watching, glacier tours, flightseeing, and downtown retail live or die on those months.
⛏️
Greens Creek & Kensington Mines
Two operating hardrock mines within the borough - Greens Creek on Admiralty Island (one of the world's largest silver mines) and Kensington to the north. Between them they sustain a year-round mining-services and marine-transport economy.
🐟
Commercial Fishing
Southeast salmon, halibut, and the processing and cold storage that goes with them - plus a charter fleet that runs out of Auke Bay all summer. Fishing is Juneau's most durable private industry.

Key Corridors & Communities

Downtown / Franklin Street
The capitol, the cruise docks, and the tourist retail core. Extremely seasonal - packed May through September, quiet the rest of the year except during session.
Mendenhall Valley
Where most Juneauites live. The Glacier Highway retail strip, contractors, clinics, and the year-round service businesses that do not depend on ships.
Douglas & West Juneau
Across the Gastineau Channel via the Douglas Bridge. Residential, small trades, and the Eaglecrest ski area.
Lemon Creek
The industrial belt. Warehouses, the freight terminals, contractors' yards, and light manufacturing - the working part of the borough.
Auke Bay
The ferry terminal and harbor. Charter operators, marine services, and the NOAA Auke Bay fisheries research lab.
Airport / Glacier Highway
Juneau International and the commercial corridor around it. Air taxis, flightseeing operators, car rental, and the hotels that catch both seasons.
Thane / South of Downtown
The road south along the channel toward the old AJ mine ruins and Sheep Creek - a small industrial and residential fringe.
Out the Road
North past Auke Bay to the end of Glacier Highway at about mile 40. Rural, remote, and still inside the borough.

Getting Around

🛣️
Egan Drive / Glacier Highway
The single spine of Juneau. Egan runs from downtown out to the valley, becomes Glacier Highway, and ends about forty miles later. That is the road system - all of it.
✈️
Juneau International Airport
The practical way in and out. Alaska Airlines jets, air taxis to the surrounding villages, and flightseeing operators serving the summer trade.
⛴️
Alaska Marine Highway
The state ferry system is Juneau's road. Vehicles, freight, and people move by ferry to Haines, Skagway, Sitka, and Ketchikan - on a schedule, not on demand.
💰 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
But Read This Anyway.

Alaska has no state sales tax and no state personal income tax — which is exactly why so many business owners get Alaska tax wrong. More than 100 local jurisdictions levy their own sales tax, and the rules change borough by borough and city by city.

Juneau: The Numbers

This is where Alaska's tax story gets counterintuitive. There is no state sales tax and no state personal income tax - but Juneau, unlike Anchorage and Fairbanks, levies a local sales tax of its own. And because Juneau is a unified city-borough, there is only one rate to worry about.

What It Means for Your Books

💵
Juneau charges 5% - Anchorage and Fairbanks charge nothing
Alaska's two biggest cities have no general sales tax, so business owners moving to or selling into Juneau routinely assume Alaska is a no-sales-tax state. It is not. The City and Borough of Juneau levies a 5% areawide sales tax, and it applies to services as well as goods - which catches consultants, contractors, and agencies off guard far more often than it catches retailers. Rate is as of 2026; confirm the current rate and any exemptions with CBJ Finance.
🗓️
Two seasons means two very different sets of books
The legislative session runs January into May; the cruise season runs roughly May into September. Most Juneau businesses earn a lopsided share of their year in one or both windows, then run lean. We build the books to show cash runway through the shoulder, handle deferred revenue on bookings taken months in advance, and track seasonal payroll properly.
🚢
Freight is a structural cost line, not an afterthought
With no road connection, every item you sell arrived by barge or plane. Landed cost - not invoice cost - is your real COGS. We set up the chart of accounts so freight and handling are visible against the products they belong to, because a Juneau margin that ignores freight is a fiction.
