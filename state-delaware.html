📍 About Delaware

Where Most of the Fortune 500 Incorporate.

Delaware's outsized economic role comes from its business-friendly legal system: more than a million entities — including a large majority of the Fortune 500 — are incorporated here, drawn by the specialized Court of Chancery. The 1981 Financial Center Development Act made Wilmington a national banking and credit-card hub, and the state retains a strong chemical and pharmaceutical legacy from DuPont, now carried by Chemours, Corteva, and Incyte. Downstate, Sussex County is one of the country's top broiler-chicken producers, and the Port of Wilmington anchors regional trade.

The DE tax picture

Delaware is famous for what it doesn't charge: there is no state or local sales tax at all. But businesses shouldn't assume it's tax-free. Instead of a sales tax, Delaware levies a gross receipts tax on a seller's total receipts — with no deductions for costs — at rates that vary by business type (roughly 0.0945% to 0.7468% for most activities, as of 2026 — confirm the current rate before filing). Incorporated entities also owe an annual franchise tax and report, which is how the nation's incorporation capital funds itself. The individual income tax is graduated up to a top rate of 6.6%. If you're incorporated in Delaware, the franchise tax and gross receipts tax — not a sales tax — are what to plan for.
