Bookkeeping Proposal

Books set up right from Viale’s first week, and payroll off your plate.

Prepared for Kate Shotwell Prepared by Dan Cope October 8, 2026

Where you’re starting

A new restaurant, a new LLC, and a clean slate.

Viale opens in Hampden as soon as next week: wood-fired pizza, salads and a full bar, counter service, with a team of 13 and growing. You’re on Toast for the register, payroll and tips, you bank at M&T, and you take cards only. Shave ice joins the menu when the weather turns.

You told me that at your last location you never got to the point of knowing what things cost, and that you don’t want to be the one running payroll. This plan sets the books up before the first sale so the numbers are useful from the start, and takes payroll preparation off your plate. Setup happens in October and monthly service starts November 1.

What we’ll build in

Four things that make a restaurant’s books useful.

01
A chart of accounts built for a pizza restaurant with a bar
Food sales and bar sales are kept apart, and so are food cost, bar cost and labor. That gives you food cost, bar cost and labor as a share of sales every month. I’ll send the chart for you to look over before anything is posted, and it can be adjusted any time.
02
Toast sales posted the right way
Each day’s sales go in as one daily summary, with sales tax, tips and card fees split out, then matched to the deposit that lands at M&T. No pile of individual tickets, and no duplicates to clean up later.
03
Tips and sales tax kept out of your income
Card tips are money you’re holding for your staff until payroll pays them every two weeks, and sales tax is money you’re holding for Maryland. Both are tracked as amounts owed, so your sales figures aren’t inflated and you can see what’s due.
04
Everything you spent getting open, recorded once
The buildout, equipment and pre-opening costs go on the books in the right places, and the money each of you put in is tracked by partner. Your CPA gets a clean starting point, including if you elect S-corp status later.

Scope of work

Set up in October, then books closed every month.

  • Setup (one-time). Your QuickBooks Online file configured, the chart of accounts built, Toast sales and payroll mapped to it, bank rules built, tips and sales tax set up, the money spent on the new LLC before opening recorded, and your first weeks of sales in October posted and checked.
  • Monthly bookkeeping, closed every month. Toast sales posted and matched to deposits, purchases categorized, your bank account reconciled, payroll recorded, tips and sales tax tracked, and the month closed with a P&L, balance sheet and a written analysis.
  • Payroll, prepared for you every two weeks. I prepare each payroll in Toast: hours, tips from Tips Manager, new hires and changes. You review and approve it, and Toast pays your team and files the payroll taxes. Billed per payroll, separate from the monthly fee.
  • Ready for your CPA. I don’t do tax returns; I’m the monthly engine that feeds your CPA. They get reconciled books and a year-end package in January. If you elect S-corp status, tell me when it takes effect and I’ll adjust the books to match.
