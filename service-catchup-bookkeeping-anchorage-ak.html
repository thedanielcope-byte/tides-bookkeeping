Catch-Up Bookkeeping for
Anchorage Businesses.

When a season outruns the books, owners end up months or years behind. Here is how catch-up bookkeeping fits the businesses driving the Anchorage economy, inside the Municipality of Anchorage.

Anchorage is a unified home-rule borough — the city and the borough are one government — and roughly 40 percent of Alaskans live inside it. When a business in Downtown, Midtown, Spenard, South Anchorage, or Eagle River goes a year without reconciling, the sheer transaction volume turns tax season into a genuine crisis. That is the backlog we clear.

The Municipality runs on logistics and energy. Ted Stevens Anchorage International is one of the world's busiest air-cargo airports, JBER anchors a defense-support economy, and oil and gas offices, hospitals, and Alaska Native regional corporations feed a deep vendor base. Freight brokers, machine shops, and staffing firms often need years of project-based revenue and multi-account activity reconstructed before a CPA will touch the file.

Contractors feel it worst. A short building season along the Glenn Highway and Seward Highway means the books get pushed to "later" from May through September, and later never comes. Tides goes back as far as needed, reconciles every account, and hands you a clean, CPA-ready package — fully remote. Want the full local picture? See our Anchorage bookkeeping services page.

The Alaska Tax Reality

Untangling Back
Alaska Sales Tax.

A backlog in Alaska is rarely just missing transactions. It is usually missing local sales-tax history too. Here is how we unwind it for a Anchorage business.

Anchorage owners usually get a small break here: the Municipality levies no general local sales tax (as of 2026 — confirm current rules), and neither does the state. So a backlog is rarely tangled up in missed local sales-tax returns for in-town sales. The exception is remote selling. If you shipped into Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, Kodiak, Bethel, or the Kenai Peninsula and crossed $100,000 in gross statewide sales, ARSSTC registration and back filings may be part of your cleanup — the 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025, so gross sales alone trigger it.

Rates and thresholds above are current to the best of our knowledge as of 2026 and are provided for general information only. Local ordinances change — confirm the current rate with your borough or city before you rely on it. Tides provides bookkeeping, not tax advice or tax filing.
