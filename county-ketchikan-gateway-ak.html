About the Area

Alaska's First
Port of Call -
and Its Rainiest.

The Ketchikan Gateway Borough sits at the southern tip of Southeast Alaska on Revillagigedo Island, and it is the first Alaska port most cruise ships reach coming up from Seattle or Vancouver. That single fact shapes almost everything about the local economy.

The borough contains the City of Ketchikan, the City of Saxman, and the unincorporated areas around them - including Ward Cove and Gravina Island, where the airport is. It calls itself the Salmon Capital of the World, and it has the commercial fleet and processing history to justify it.

Today the economy runs on cruise tourism, commercial fishing, the Tongass National Forest (whose headquarters are here), and the Ketchikan Shipyard. And it runs on a sales tax structure that is genuinely one of the most confusing in the state.

History & Economic Identity

Ketchikan started as a Tlingit fish camp at the mouth of Ketchikan Creek, became a salmon cannery town around 1900, and at its peak claimed to be the Salmon Capital of the World with more canneries than anywhere else on earth. Creek Street - the boardwalk on pilings over the creek - was the town's red-light district for decades and is now its most photographed block.

Timber replaced salmon as the dominant industry when the Ketchikan Pulp Company mill opened at Ward Cove in 1954, and for forty years the mill defined the town. It closed in 1997, and Ketchikan spent the following decades rebuilding its economy around cruise tourism. Today Ward Cove itself has been redeveloped as a cruise berth - the mill site turned into a dock.

The result is an economy with a very sharp shape. Roughly a million cruise passengers arrive between spring and early fall, and the downtown retail, jewelry, tour, and flightseeing businesses earn most of their year in that window. What keeps the lights on the rest of the year is the durable stuff: the commercial fishing fleet, the Ketchikan Shipyard, and the U.S. Forest Service, whose Tongass National Forest headquarters sits here in the largest national forest in the country.

💰 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
But Read This Anyway.

Alaska has no state sales tax and no state personal income tax — which is exactly why so many business owners get Alaska tax wrong. More than 100 local jurisdictions levy their own sales tax, and the rules change borough by borough and city by city.

Ketchikan Gateway Borough: The Numbers

Ketchikan may have the most complicated sales tax structure in Alaska. The rate depends on where you are (city, borough, or Saxman), what season it is, and even what kind of transaction it is. Getting it right is not optional and it is not easy.

Alaska State Sales Tax None
Borough Rate (outside city limits) 2.5%
Ketchikan City - Apr 1 to Sep 30 8.0% combined
Ketchikan City - Oct 1 to Mar 31 5.5% combined
Residential Rent (in city) 5.5% year-round
City of Saxman 6.5%

Rates shown are as of 2026 and can change. Always confirm the current rate with the taxing jurisdiction before you rely on it. Tides keeps your books tax-ready and works alongside your CPA — we do not file returns for you.

What It Means for Your Books

🗓️
The city rate jumps 2.5 points on April 1
Inside the City of Ketchikan, the combined city-plus-borough rate on non-residential sales is 8.0% from April 1 through September 30 and 5.5% from October 1 through March 31. Residential rent stays at 5.5% year-round regardless of season. Outside city limits, the borough rate alone is 2.5%. Saxman has its own rate of 6.5%. Rates are as of 2026 - confirm with the Ketchikan Gateway Borough before you rely on them.
📍
Where the sale happens changes the rate
A tour that departs from downtown, a lodge out on North Tongass, and a shop in Saxman are all in the same borough and all charge different rates. If you operate in more than one of those places - and plenty of Ketchikan businesses do - each location needs to be tracked separately in the books. We set it up that way from day one.
🚢
One season has to pay for twelve months
Cruise passengers arrive between roughly April and September and then stop. Downtown retail, tour operators, and flightseeing companies do the overwhelming majority of their business in that window. We handle deferred revenue on advance bookings, track seasonal payroll properly, and build a cash view that shows you the winter runway - not just the summer high.
🌐
Selling into Alaska from outside? ARSSTC
If you are a remote seller shipping into Ketchikan - or into any of the 100-plus Alaska jurisdictions that levy a local sales tax - the Alaska Remote Sellers Sales Tax Commission (ARSSTC) provides single-point registration and filing. The threshold is $100,000 in gross statewide sales in the current or prior calendar year. The old 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025, so transaction count no longer matters.
