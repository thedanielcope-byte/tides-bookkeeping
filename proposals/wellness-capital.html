Where you’re starting

A new acquisition, and one clean shot at setting the books up right.

You’ve just bought the spa, and the books are a blank page — a brand-new QuickBooks Online file with a fresh chart of accounts. That is the best possible starting position. Most of this work usually begins by unwinding someone else’s decisions; here there’s nothing to undo. You’re standing up Gusto for payroll, Stripe for payments, and Booker for scheduling — and I walk you through every one of them, start to finish, until all three are configured, connected to QuickBooks, and 100% ready to go. You won’t be figuring any of it out on your own.

The 2025 numbers give us the starting line: roughly $380,000 in revenue and $26,000 of net profit, with the spa currently running near break-even at about $33,000 a month. Revenue is holding up. Profit isn’t — and a few lines in that P&L don’t add up. Those are worth settling before the first month closes, not a year from now. You’re running the spa day to day, so the numbers need to keep up with you.

What we found

Three things worth settling before the first close.

01
Merchant fees above market
$20,000 in card processing on $380,000 of sales runs about 5.3%. Standard Stripe pricing puts that near $12,000. Some is likely card tips inflating the base; the rest is recoverable.
02
Gift cards and prepaid packages
California gift certificates never expire, and unredeemed service packages are a real obligation. If those balances didn’t come across onto the opening balance sheet, the spa owes more than the books show.
03
Revenue up, profit down
The spa netted $26,000 on $380,000 in 2025 and is running near break-even now on a higher run rate. The expense detail I’ve seen accounts for about $41,000 less than the full picture — the complete P&L will show where it went.
