About the Area

Russian America,
Seafood, and a
Seasonal Tax Rate.

The City and Borough of Sitka is a unified city-borough - one government covering the city and the surrounding borough on Baranof Island. It is one of the largest municipalities in the United States by land area and one of the smallest by population, which tells you most of what you need to know about Southeast Alaska.

Sitka was the capital of Russian America, and the handover ceremony that transferred Alaska to the United States happened here on Castle Hill in 1867. The Russian legacy is still visible - St. Michael's Cathedral sits in the middle of the main street - and it is a genuine driver of the visitor economy.

The working economy is built on three things: commercial fishing and seafood processing, healthcare (SEARHC is the largest employer), and a fast-growing cruise tourism trade. There is no road connection to anywhere - you arrive by plane or ferry, and so does everything you sell.

History & Economic Identity

Sitka - or New Archangel, as the Russians called it - was the capital of Russian America and, in its day, one of the busiest ports on the Pacific coast. On October 18, 1867, the Russian flag came down on Castle Hill and the American flag went up, and Alaska changed hands. Sitka stayed the capital until 1906, when the seat of government moved to Juneau.

The Tlingit were here long before either, and the Battle of Sitka in 1804 - fought where Sitka National Historical Park now stands - was one of the last major armed resistances by Alaska Natives to Russian colonization. That history is not a museum exhibit here; it is the living identity of the community.

The modern economy rests on three pillars. Seafood - Sitka is a major salmon, halibut, black cod, and herring port, with processors and cold storage that run year-round. Healthcare - SEARHC, the SouthEast Alaska Regional Health Consortium, operates the Mt. Edgecumbe Medical Center and is by a wide margin the largest employer in the borough. And tourism, which changed character dramatically when the Sitka Sound cruise terminal opened up the town to large ships. That last shift is why the seasonal sales tax matters so much more than it used to.

🐟
Seafood & Processing
Salmon, halibut, black cod, and herring. Sitka's processors and cold storage run year-round and anchor an economy of tenders, gear suppliers, marine mechanics, and permit holders.
🏥
SEARHC - The Largest Employer
The SouthEast Alaska Regional Health Consortium runs the Mt. Edgecumbe Medical Center on Japonski Island and employs more people than any other organization in the borough - supporting a cluster of medical, dental, and allied-health practices.
🚢
Cruise Tourism
The Sitka Sound cruise terminal transformed the summer economy. Tour operators, wildlife guides, retail, and restaurants now see a passenger surge that concentrates a large share of the year's revenue into a few months.
⛪
Russian America Heritage
St. Michael's Cathedral, Castle Hill, the Russian Bishop's House, and Sitka National Historical Park. The history is a real economic asset - it is why the ships stop here.

💰 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
But Read This Anyway.

Alaska has no state sales tax and no state personal income tax — which is exactly why so many business owners get Alaska tax wrong. More than 100 local jurisdictions levy their own sales tax, and the rules change borough by borough and city by city.

Sitka: The Numbers

Sitka has one of the trickiest sales tax rules in Alaska: the rate changes twice a year. If your point-of-sale system is not set to flip on the right dates, you are either over-collecting or under-collecting for six months at a stretch.

What It Means for Your Books

🗓️
The rate flips on April 1 and October 1
The City and Borough of Sitka runs a seasonal sales tax: 6% during the second and third quarters (April 1 through September 30) and 5% during the fourth and first quarters (October 1 through March 31). It is a small difference that compounds - a shop doing meaningful volume through the cruise season and not flipping the rate on time will be off by real money by the time the return is due. Rates are as of 2026; confirm with CBS Finance.
🚢
Landed cost is your real cost
Sitka has no road connection. Every item of inventory arrived by barge or plane, and the freight is not a footnote - it is part of your true COGS. We set the chart of accounts up so freight sits against the goods it belongs to, because margin math that ignores barge freight is fiction in Southeast Alaska.
🐟
Fisheries taxes stack on top
If you catch, buy, or process fish, Alaska's fisheries business tax and fishery resource landing tax may apply in addition to local sales tax. Fish tickets, crew shares, IFQ costs, and gear depreciation all need to be handled correctly. We keep it organized so your CPA can file confidently.
