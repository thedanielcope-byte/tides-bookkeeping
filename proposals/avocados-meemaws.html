Where you’re starting

Two restaurants, one set of books to build.

You took over Avocados in April and opened Mee Maw’s Country Kitchen on August 13. Avocados is running roughly $15,000–$20,000 a week — about $100,000 a month — on Peach State Bank, with eight people on QuickBooks Payroll. Mee Maw’s is doing around $20,000 a month on breakfast alone, with three on payroll and a Bank of America account, and you’re expanding into lunch. Both are moving onto Toast.

None of that is a problem. What you don’t have yet is a set of books that can prove any of it. The previous owner of Avocados left you almost nothing to work from, and Mee Maw’s doesn’t have a QuickBooks file at all. Meanwhile there’s an insurance audit next April, a CPA meeting on October 22, a third location you’re evaluating next week, and a stated goal of ten locations in eighteen months.

This is the right moment to fix it. Setting the structure up correctly across two restaurants costs a fraction of untangling it across ten.

What we found

Five things that need to be handled now.

01
Avocados has no usable history since the April takeover
The previous owner handed over the restaurant but not the records. Five months — April through August — have to be rebuilt from bank activity, and the opening balance tied back to the $30,000 left in the Peach State Bank account at closing.
02
Mee Maw’s has no books at all
Open since August 13 with no QuickBooks company file. The $5,000 owner contribution, the first weeks of sales, and the payroll already run are sitting unrecorded. Every week that passes is another week to reconstruct later.
03
Two restaurants, no common chart of accounts
Without one restaurant-standard chart of accounts across both files, the two P&Ls won’t compare to each other — and folding in a third location gets harder every month you wait.
04
Weekly payroll and weekly tips, with an insurance audit next April
11 people across two entities, paid every Friday, tips out the same day. Tip allocation, wage coding and payroll tax filings need to be right now — not reconstructed under audit pressure.
05
No owner compensation structure in place
You’re 100% owner of both entities and drawing to a separate LLC with nothing formalized. Percentage-based distributions need to be set up and tracked cleanly before the CPA meeting on October 22.

Scope of work

What I’ll actually do.

  • Catch-up and cleanup. Avocados rebuilt and fully reconciled from April 1 through August 31, 2026, with opening balances tied to the purchase. Mee Maw’s QuickBooks Online built from scratch — company file, restaurant chart of accounts, payroll and bank feeds — with August 13–31 recorded.
  • Ongoing weekly bookkeeping. Both files maintained every week: bank and debit-card reconciliation, Toast daily sales entries, vendor invoices and checks coded, payroll and tips posted, owner distributions tracked. Monthly P&L, balance sheet and cash flow for each location, in the same format so they read side by side.
  • CPA coordination. You walk into the October 22 meeting with closed books and a handoff package instead of a shoebox. Tides is the monthly engine that feeds your CPA — not a replacement for one. I’ll join that call if it’s useful.
