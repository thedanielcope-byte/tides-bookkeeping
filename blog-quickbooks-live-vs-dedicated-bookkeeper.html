QuickBooks Live vs. a Dedicated Bookkeeper: Which Should You Choose?

T Tides Bookkeeping · July 31, 2026 · 9 min read

If you use QuickBooks Online, Intuit has almost certainly offered you QuickBooks Live — its in-app bookkeeping service — right inside the software. The pitch is compelling: the biggest name in small-business accounting, a bookkeeper attached to the file you already use, predictable pricing. For some businesses it's genuinely the right call. For others, the limits of the model show up at exactly the moment the books start to matter. Here's an honest comparison between QuickBooks Live and hiring a dedicated bookkeeping firm — including where we'd tell you to pick Intuit.

What QuickBooks Live is (and what it costs)

As of 2026, QuickBooks Live comes in two very different flavors:

  • Expert Assisted (~$50/month). An advice line, not a bookkeeping service. Intuit's experts answer questions and guide you while you keep the books yourself. Genuinely useful for confident DIY owners who occasionally get stuck.
  • Full-Service Bookkeeping (roughly $300–$700/month). Intuit's team does the books for you — categorization, bank reconciliation, and monthly reports — priced by your monthly expenses (about $300 up to $10k/month in expenses, $500 to $50k, $700 above that), typically after a one-time cleanup fee starting around $800.

Two costs sit on top of those numbers: your QuickBooks Online subscription itself (roughly $35–$235/month depending on plan) is separate, and anything outside the core scope — invoicing, bill pay, payroll administration, catch-up beyond the initial cleanup — generally isn't included. Prices and scope are Intuit's to change, so verify current terms before you buy.

Where QuickBooks Live is genuinely strong

  • Simplicity of trust. It's Intuit. The company isn't disappearing, the service lives inside software you already use, and there's no new vendor relationship to manage.
  • The advice tier is cheap. At ~$50/month, Expert Assisted is one of the cheapest ways to get a professional sanity-check on DIY books.
  • Fine for genuinely simple books. A low-volume service business with one bank account, no inventory, no contractors, and no sales-tax complexity will usually be well served.

Where the model falls short

A team is not a bookkeeper. QuickBooks Live assigns you a primary bookkeeper backed by an extended team, with communication mostly through scheduled video calls and in-app messaging. That works until your situation needs context — a workflow quirk, an industry wrinkle, a history only someone who's done your books for two years would know. Continuity is the single biggest practical difference between the models.

The scope is the scope. Categorization, reconciliation, reports. If you need accounts payable, invoicing help, payroll coordination, per-job or per-property tracking, or someone to chase down why the POS doesn't match the bank — that's outside the box. Businesses in complex industries (restaurants, contractors, real estate investors, e-commerce) tend to hit these walls first.

Cash-basis by default, complexity by exception. Live bookkeeping is built around straightforward cash-basis books. Accrual books, inventory, and messy multi-channel revenue are exactly where generalist-pool models strain.

The price gap is smaller than it looks. Once you're in the $500–$700 tier plus a QBO subscription, you're paying what a dedicated firm charges — without the dedicated part.

What a dedicated bookkeeping firm looks like

A dedicated firm — ours or anyone's worth hiring — gives you one named bookkeeper who owns your file month after month, works inside your QuickBooks Online subscription (so your data stays yours), knows your industry's specific mechanics, and flexes scope as you grow: AP/AR, payroll coordination, job costing, sales-tax-ready records, and real catch-up work when you're behind. For calibration, our own plans run $299–$895/month flat, tiered by expense volume — the same territory as QuickBooks Live's full-service tiers, with a materially different depth of service. Industry-wide, most dedicated flat-rate engagements land between $300 and $1,000/month; our bookkeeper cost guide breaks the market down.

The clean way to frame the decision: QuickBooks Live sells you bookkeeping tasks. A dedicated firm sells you a bookkeeper. The tasks are 80% of the price; the person is the other 80% of the value.

Which should you choose?

Choose QuickBooks Live if: your books are simple and low-volume, you're comfortable with a team-and-video-call service model, you mostly need the basics done reliably — or you're a DIY owner who just wants the $50 advice tier as a safety net. It is a legitimate product at a fair price for that profile.

Choose a dedicated bookkeeper if: your industry has real mechanics (COGS, jobs, properties, tips, multi-state sales tax), you're behind and need catch-up, you want one accountable person who knows your business, or you're growing toward loans, investors, or an eventual sale — situations where the quality and continuity of your books directly convert to money. Our guide to the seven signs it's time to hire a bookkeeper maps this line in detail, and bookkeeper vs. CPA vs. accountant covers who does what above it.

Whichever you pick, the software underneath is likely the same — and that's fine. Keep your books in your own QuickBooks Online file, keep your data portable, and the decision stays reversible. For the full lay of the land, start with our complete guide to small business bookkeeping.
