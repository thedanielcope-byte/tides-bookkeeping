🧾 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
100+ Local Ones.

Alaska is one of the very few states with no statewide sales tax and no state personal income tax. But that headline hides the part that actually hits your books: more than 100 boroughs and cities levy their own sales tax — roughly 0% to 7.5% — each with its own rate, exemptions, caps, and filing calendar. Rates below are as of 2026; always confirm the current rate with your borough or city.

What Alaska Does Not Tax

The two taxes most business owners expect to fight simply are not there — which is genuinely great news, and also the reason so many Alaska owners get blindsided by the taxes that are.

🚫
No Statewide Sales Tax
Alaska levies no state-level sales tax. There is no state rate to collect, no state sales tax return, and no state sales tax permit. Everything happens at the borough and city level instead.
🧍
No State Personal Income Tax
Alaska repealed its personal income tax in 1980 and has not reenacted it. Owners of pass-through entities — sole proprietors, partnerships, S-corps — owe no Alaska income tax on that business income.
🏙️
Anchorage and Fairbanks: No General Sales Tax Either
Counterintuitive but true — the Municipality of Anchorage and the Fairbanks North Star Borough do not impose a general local sales tax. Two of Alaska's biggest markets are sales-tax-free, even while Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, Kodiak, and Bethel all collect.

What Alaska Does Tax

🏛️
100+ Local Borough & City Sales Taxes (about 0%–7.5%)
Each jurisdiction writes its own code. Rates, what is exempt, whether there is a cap on the taxable amount of a single sale, and how often you file all change the moment you cross a borough line. If you operate in more than one community, you are effectively running more than one sales tax compliance program.
🌐
ARSSTC — One Filing for Remote Sellers
The Alaska Remote Sellers Sales Tax Commission lets remote sellers and marketplace facilitators register and file once, statewide, instead of with dozens of individual towns. The threshold is $100,000 in gross statewide sales in the current or prior calendar year — the old 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025.
🏢
Corporate Income Tax (C-Corporations)
Alaska does tax C-corporation net income, on graduated brackets that top out around 9.4%. Pass-through owners are not hit by it — but if you are incorporated as a C-corp, it is a real return with real numbers behind it.
🐟
Fisheries Business Tax & Industry Levies
Seafood processors and exporters face the fisheries business tax, and resource industries carry their own production and severance taxes. If you are anywhere near fish, oil, or minerals, your books need to be built for it.

Why This Is a Bookkeeping Problem, Not Just a Tax Problem

You cannot file a borough sales tax return correctly if your books do not separate revenue by where the sale actually happened. A charter operator running trips out of Homer and Seward, a contractor bidding jobs in Palmer and Anchorage, an online seller shipping statewide — each needs revenue tagged by jurisdiction from the moment it is recorded, not reconstructed in a panic at filing time. That is the work we do.

📍 About Alaska

A State That Runs on Oil,
Fish, and Freight.

Alaska's economy looks like nowhere else in America: North Slope oil moving down the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, one of the world's great commercial fishing and seafood-processing industries, a tourism season that packs most of a year's revenue into five months, major military installations, and Ted Stevens Anchorage International — one of the busiest cargo airports on the planet. And nearly everything a business sells here arrived by barge, plane, or pipeline.

The Interior hub — the University of Alaska Fairbanks, Fort Wainwright and Eielson Air Force Base, gold mining, the Trans-Alaska Pipeline corridor, and a winter aurora-tourism season that fills hotels at forty below.

Alaska's Economy & Business Climate

Alaska is a resource state with a services economy layered on top. Oil and gas still underwrite much of the public budget, commercial fishing feeds a global seafood market, and tourism, the military, mining, and Alaska Native corporations round out the base. Anchorage is the commercial center; Juneau is the seat of government; Fairbanks anchors the Interior.

The bookkeeping reality behind all of it is freight. Almost every input a business uses got here by barge, plane, or truck, and shipping is often one of the largest lines in the P&L. If freight is not tracked properly, your landed cost is wrong, your margins are fiction, and you cannot tell a bad month from a bad price. That — plus seasonal revenue and borough-level sales tax — is what Alaska books have to get right.

🛢️
Oil & Gas — North Slope, TAPS, Cook Inlet
North Slope production feeding the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System, plus Cook Inlet's older fields near Kenai — driving a deep bench of service companies, contractors, logistics firms, and camp support businesses across the state.
🎣
Commercial Fishing & Seafood Processing
Kodiak, Ketchikan, Sitka, Homer, and the Bering Sea fleet make Alaska one of the world's largest seafood producers. Season-driven revenue, crew shares, and the fisheries business tax make these books genuinely different from a mainland business.
🚢
Tourism & Cruise
Cruise ships pour passengers into Juneau, Ketchikan, and Sitka all summer while lodges, guides, and charter operators run flat out — then the town goes quiet. Five months of revenue has to carry twelve months of overhead.
🎖️
Military — JBER, Eielson, Fort Wainwright, USCG Kodiak
Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Eielson Air Force Base and Fort Wainwright around Fairbanks, and Coast Guard Base Kodiak anchor stable, year-round payrolls — and a steady stream of contractors and subcontractors around them.
✈️
Air Cargo & Aviation — Ted Stevens Anchorage International
Anchorage sits within about nine and a half hours of most of the industrialized world, which is why it ranks among the busiest cargo airports anywhere. Add the bush-aviation network that serves communities with no roads, and aviation is core Alaska infrastructure.
⛏️
Mining, Government & Alaska Native Corporations
Zinc, gold, and silver mines across the state; state and local government as a top employer, especially in Juneau; and the ANCSA regional and village corporations, which are among Alaska's largest and most economically significant businesses.

Getting Around: Roads, Air, and Water

Alaska has a handful of highways and an enormous amount of everything else. The state ferry and the airplane do the work the interstate does elsewhere — and that shows up directly in what you spend to move goods and people.

Glenn Highway (AK-1)
Anchorage to Palmer and Wasilla — the Mat-Su commuter corridor and the state's busiest stretch of road.
Seward Highway (AK-1/9)
Anchorage south along Turnagain Arm to the Kenai Peninsula — fishing, charters, and summer tourism traffic.
Parks Highway (AK-3)
Wasilla to Fairbanks past Denali — the Southcentral-to-Interior freight and tourism link.
Richardson & Steese Highways
Fairbanks to Valdez and out into the Interior, shadowing the Trans-Alaska Pipeline corridor.
Alaska Marine Highway System
The state ferry — and, for most of Southeast and much of the coast, the actual highway. Freight, vehicles, and people all ride it.
Air Is Infrastructure
Juneau and Bethel have no road connection to the rest of Alaska. Air and water freight are not a convenience here — they are the supply chain, and they are a line item.

Freight: The Line Item Everyone Underestimates

Shipping, barge, and air-freight costs can move a product's true cost by double digits — and they rarely arrive on the same invoice as the goods. We categorize freight to the right accounts so your cost of goods sold reflects what it actually cost to get the thing to Alaska, not just what the vendor charged for it. That one habit changes what your margins look like — and what you decide to charge.
