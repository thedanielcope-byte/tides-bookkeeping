📍 About Texas

The Lone Star State:
Built for Business.

Home to roughly 30 million residents — the 2nd-most populous state in the country — Texas has no state income tax, the 2nd-largest GDP in the nation, and one of the most business-friendly climates anywhere in America. From the energy capital of the world in Houston to the tech explosion in Austin and the massive DFW Metroplex, every region of Texas is a different economic engine.

🛢️
Houston & Gulf Coast
Houston · Pasadena · Baytown · Galveston
The energy capital of the world — home to more Fortune 500 headquarters than any city except New York. Houston anchors oil and gas, the Texas Medical Center (the world's largest), the Port of Houston (largest in the US by tonnage), and NASA's Johnson Space Center.
🏙️
Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex
Dallas · Fort Worth · Arlington · Plano · Frisco · Irving
The 4th-largest metro area in the United States. DFW is a powerhouse of finance, telecommunications, defense (Lockheed Martin, Raytheon), logistics (DFW Airport), and corporate relocations — with more companies moving their headquarters here every year.
💻
Austin & Central Texas
Austin · Round Rock · San Marcos · Cedar Park
Silicon Hills — Texas's tech capital. Home to Tesla's Gigafactory, Samsung's chip fab, Dell, Oracle, and hundreds of startups. Austin also hosts the state capital, UT Austin, and a massive live-music and hospitality economy anchored by SXSW and ACL.
🏛️
San Antonio, West TX & the Valley
San Antonio · El Paso · Laredo · Corpus Christi · Lubbock
San Antonio brings military (Joint Base San Antonio, Fort Cavazos) and tourism (the Alamo, River Walk). El Paso anchors the US-Mexico border economy. Lubbock and the Permian Basin drive oil, gas, and agriculture. Laredo is the busiest inland port in the Western Hemisphere.

Texas Economy & Business Climate

Texas is one of the most business-friendly states in America — with no state income tax, no personal income tax, and a regulatory environment that has spent decades attracting employers and entrepreneurs. If Texas were a country, its economy would rank as the 8th-largest in the world. The result is a magnetic effect: thousands of businesses relocate to Texas every year, and the state's population continues to grow faster than nearly anywhere else in the country.

Texas became the 28th state in 1845 (after a decade as an independent republic) and is the only state that borders both Mexico and the Gulf of Mexico while spanning plains, deserts, forests, and coastline. Its modern economy is anchored by energy (oil, natural gas, wind, solar), technology, healthcare, defense, agriculture (cattle, cotton, produce), manufacturing, and international trade.

🛢️
Energy Capital of the World
Houston is the global headquarters for oil and gas — ExxonMobil, ConocoPhillips, Phillips 66, Halliburton, and dozens more. The Permian Basin in West Texas produces more oil than most countries. Texas also leads the nation in wind energy generation.
🏥
Texas Medical Center — World's Largest
Houston's Texas Medical Center is the largest medical complex on Earth — 60+ institutions, including MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston Methodist, and Baylor College of Medicine. Over 10 million patient encounters per year and a massive ecosystem of healthcare small businesses.
💻
Silicon Hills — Austin's Tech Boom
Tesla, Oracle, Samsung, Dell, Apple's $1B campus, Meta, Google, and hundreds of startups call Austin home. The city's tech sector has exploded, making it one of the fastest-growing tech hubs in North America alongside the DFW tech corridor.
🪖
Military & Defense — Fort Cavazos, Fort Bliss, JBSA
Texas has more military installations than any other state. Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood) is the largest active-duty armored post in the world. Joint Base San Antonio and Fort Bliss (El Paso) drive billions in defense spending and support thousands of contractor small businesses.
🚢
Port of Houston & International Trade
The Port of Houston is the largest port in the US by tonnage and a top container port. Laredo is the busiest inland port in the Western Hemisphere for US-Mexico trade. Texas's location makes it a logistics and trade gateway for North America.

Key Interstate & Highway Infrastructure

Texas has more highway miles than any other state — over 80,000 miles connecting metros, oil fields, border crossings, and ports. The interstate system ties together a state that spans 770 miles east-to-west and 800 miles north-to-south.

I-35 — NAFTA Superhighway
Laredo to San Antonio to Austin to Dallas-Fort Worth — the primary north-south trade corridor linking Mexico to the US heartland. The busiest freight route in Texas.
I-10 — Coast to West Texas
Houston to San Antonio to El Paso — spanning the entire southern width of Texas. Connects the Gulf Coast energy sector to the Permian Basin and the US-Mexico border at El Paso.
I-45 — Houston to Dallas
Connects Texas's two largest metros — Houston and Dallas-Fort Worth — through Huntsville and Corsicana. One of the busiest and most economically important corridors in the state.
I-20 — East-West Corridor
Runs from the Louisiana border through Dallas-Fort Worth, Abilene, Midland-Odessa, and into West Texas — connecting the Permian Basin oil fields to the DFW Metroplex and beyond.
I-30 — Dallas to Texarkana
Links Dallas-Fort Worth to the Arkansas border, running through Arlington (home of AT&T Stadium and Globe Life Field) and connecting to the broader Southeast.
US-59 / I-69 — Houston to the Valley
Houston south through Victoria to the Rio Grande Valley and Laredo — the artery connecting the Gulf Coast to the border economy and one of the fastest-growing trade routes in Texas.

TX Landmarks & Cultural Identity

🤠
The Alamo & San Antonio River Walk
The Alamo is the most iconic landmark in Texas and one of the most visited sites in the US. The River Walk draws millions of tourists annually, driving San Antonio's massive hospitality, restaurant, and convention economy.
🎓
UT Austin, Texas A&M & Rice
The University of Texas at Austin, Texas A&M (College Station), and Rice University (Houston) anchor a massive higher-ed system — fueling Texas's healthcare, tech, engineering, energy, and research workforce.
🚀
NASA Johnson Space Center & SpaceX Starbase
Houston's Johnson Space Center has been the home of human spaceflight since the Apollo era. SpaceX's Starbase in Boca Chica (near Brownsville) is the launch site for Starship — the most powerful rocket ever built.
