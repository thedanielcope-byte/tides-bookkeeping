📍 About Pennsylvania

Eds, Meds, and Marcellus Shale.

Pennsylvania's economy leans on "eds and meds" — a dense network of major hospital systems, health insurers, and universities that are among the state's largest employers. Finance, insurance, and professional services carry a large share of the economy, alongside a manufacturing base with deep steel and industrial roots. Agriculture is significant too (Pennsylvania is the nation's top mushroom producer), and Marcellus Shale has made the state the second-largest natural gas producer in the country.

The PA tax picture

Pennsylvania's personal income tax is unusually simple: a flat 3.07% on nearly all income, with no brackets and no standard deduction (as of 2026 — confirm current rate). The catch is local. Most Pennsylvania municipalities and school districts levy a local Earned Income Tax (EIT) on wages — commonly around 1% — and Philadelphia runs its own City Wage Tax of roughly 3.75% for residents, so the true income tax burden depends heavily on where you live and work. The state sales tax is 6%, with Philadelphia adding 2% (for 8%) and Allegheny County/Pittsburgh adding 1% (for 7%). Corporations pay the Corporate Net Income Tax, which is phasing down each year — about 7.49% in 2026, on a scheduled path toward 4.99% by 2031 (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). Between the flat state rate and a patchwork of local taxes, clean payroll and location tracking really matter here.
