Books for Triangle Premier Properties, built right from day one.

Where you’re starting

A brokerage on its own for the first time. Books that haven’t started yet.

You left Compass and reopened Triangle Premier Properties under your own name, and Steve came with you. You’ve got SkySlope running transactions and commission paperwork, a Wells Fargo checking account and a business card, and a CPA who has already moved you to an S-corp. What you don’t have yet is a set of books.

Nine months of 2026 exists only on bank and card statements. Last year’s return is filed and behind you, so this is the cleanest kind of start: build QuickBooks from scratch, reconstruct January through September, and go live on a monthly routine October 1. Nothing to unwind, and no more rebuilding the year from scratch every April.

What we found

Four things to set up before the year closes.

01
There’s no accounting system yet
Everything from 2026 lives in Wells Fargo and your card statements. QuickBooks Online Simple Start is all you need — the chart of accounts gets built around what a brokerage actually has: commission income, agent splits, desk and transaction fees, and the marketing and systems spend you run. Built once, correctly, so it still works when you’re at fifteen agents.
02
You’re an S-corp and haven’t taken a salary this year
Your CPA moved you to an S-corp, and that means reasonable compensation has to be on the books before December 31. He sets the number; I get payroll running and record it so it lands on this year’s return instead of becoming next year’s problem. This is the one item on the page with a real deadline.
03
SkySlope handles the deal. It doesn’t handle the books.
SkySlope tracks the transaction, the disbursement and the splits, and it’s good at that. Your books still live in QuickBooks either way. You were quoted an accounting migration on top of it — you don’t need it. One closing note per deal and the split gets coded here, at no extra charge.
04
Steve’s cap isn’t being tracked anywhere
He pays a $400 monthly desk fee plus a transaction fee that stops once he hits his cap, and nothing is keeping a running total today. A saved report gives you each agent’s fees line by line, so you know the month someone caps out before they tell you — and it holds up as you recruit.

Scope of work

Build it, catch up nine months, then keep it current.

  • Setup and catch-up (one-time). QuickBooks Online set up from scratch with a chart of accounts built for a brokerage. Your Wells Fargo checking account and business card connected, January 1 through September 30 reconstructed from statements and reconciled, and your S-corp equity accounts set up the way your CPA needs them.
  • Monthly bookkeeping, closed every month. Commission wires from the closing attorneys recorded and split by agent, Steve’s desk fee and transaction fees tracked against his cap, expenses categorized, both accounts reconciled as soon as statements land, and the month closed with a P&L and balance sheet you can read in five minutes.
  • Ready for your CPA. I don’t do tax returns and I’m not replacing him; I’m the monthly engine that feeds him. He gets reconciled books, a year-end package in January, and a salary figure already recorded — plus the 1099s for Steve and anyone else you pay.
