📍 About Oregon

No Sales Tax. Home of the Silicon Forest.

Oregon's economy blends high tech, apparel, and natural resources. The Portland-area "Silicon Forest" is home to Intel's largest research and manufacturing campus and a deep semiconductor cluster, while Beaverton hosts Nike's global headquarters alongside Adidas North America and Columbia Sportswear. Timber and forestry remain major industries, and the Willamette Valley's agriculture and pinot noir wine country round out a diverse economy.

The OR tax picture

Oregon's headline fact is that it has no state sales tax — one of only a handful of states with none. It makes up for it with income taxes: the personal income tax is steeply graduated, running up to a top rate around 9.9% (as of 2026 — confirm current rates). Oregon also levies a statewide Corporate Activity Tax (CAT), a gross-receipts-style business tax equal to $250 plus 0.57% of taxable Oregon commercial activity above $1 million, so higher-revenue businesses owe it even in a low-profit year (as of 2026 — confirm current thresholds). On top of that, the Portland metro area has its own local income taxes (such as Multnomah County's Preschool for All and Metro's Supportive Housing Services tax). No sales tax is a real advantage; the income-side taxes are where Oregon businesses need clean books.
