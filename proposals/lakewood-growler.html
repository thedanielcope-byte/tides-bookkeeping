Bookkeeping Proposal

Books for Lakewood Growler that keep themselves current, without the manual work.

Where you’re starting

A steady, well-run bar. A bookkeeping process that still runs on downloads.

Lakewood Growler has been pouring Texas craft beer since 2014. Sales run about $35,000 a month, you did $418,000 in 2025, and this year is tracking higher as you get back to pre-COVID numbers. You’ve got a small team, DigitalPour running the taps, Clover at the register, and Patriot for payroll.

The books themselves are in good shape and current through June 30. What isn’t working is everything around them. Stacie downloads the Clover and merchant fee reports and the card statements, someone else types them in by hand, and your tax returns wait until the last minute every year even though everything is in by January. The plan below catches up July through September, connects Clover to QuickBooks, and has you on a clean monthly routine starting October 1.

What we found

Four things to fix, and to build in going forward.

01
Clover has never been connected to QuickBooks
Every month, Stacie pulls the POS report, the merchant fee report and two card statements into Google Drive, and they get entered by hand. Connecting Clover sends each day’s sales into QuickBooks on its own. I still check it against Clover’s reports so nothing is missed, but nobody on your side has to download or type anything.
02
A deposit isn’t the same as a day’s sales
Clover pays out after card fees come out, and the same deposit mixes beer sales, sales tax and any card tips. Each piece gets its own place: sales at the full amount, fees as an expense, sales tax as money held for Texas, and tips as money owed to staff. Your sales and your costs are both right, and they tie to what you report.
03
Your returns shouldn’t wait until fall
You had everything to your accountant by the end of January, and you’re still waiting on returns in September. With the books closed every month, a year-end package is ready in January: reconciled statements, payroll totals, and each partner’s distributions. Whoever does your returns next can start right away instead of starting from scratch.
04
Beer cost you can actually compare to beer sales
DigitalPour already knows every keg you tap. In the books, keg deposits are tracked as deposits, not as beer cost, and draft, crowlers and growlers, and merchandise each land in their own line. That makes it clear month to month what you’re spending on beer against what it brings in.
