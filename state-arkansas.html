📍 About Arkansas

Home of Walmart, Tyson Foods, and J.B. Hunt.

Arkansas punches far above its weight in corporate America — it's home to three Fortune 500 headquarters clustered in the northwest: Walmart in Bentonville, the world's largest retailer; Tyson Foods in Springdale, a poultry and food-processing giant; and J.B. Hunt in Lowell, a transportation and logistics leader. Beyond that corridor, the economy runs on agriculture (rice, poultry, and soybeans), manufacturing, steel production in the northeast, and aerospace and defense.

The AR tax picture

Arkansas has been cutting income taxes aggressively — the top individual rate has dropped several times in recent years and stands at roughly 3.7% as of 2026 (confirm the current rate before filing), down from higher levels. Sales tax is where the burden shifts: the state rate is 6.5%, and with city and county add-ons the combined average reaches about 9.4%, among the higher rates nationally. Arkansas has also been reducing its tax on groceries. For business owners, the falling income-tax rate is a genuine tailwind, but local sales-tax tracking still requires attention.
