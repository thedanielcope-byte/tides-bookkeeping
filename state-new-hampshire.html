📍 About New Hampshire

Live Free or Die — No Income Tax, No Sales Tax.

New Hampshire's economy blends advanced manufacturing, technology and defense, healthcare, and tourism drawn to the White Mountains, lakes, and seacoast. Its southern tier — Manchester, Nashua, and the Salem and Derry corridor — sits within Greater Boston's orbit and hosts finance, biotech, and professional services, while the Live Free or Die ethos and absence of sales and income taxes attract entrepreneurs and cross-border shoppers. Small businesses make up a large share of the state's employers.

The NH tax picture

New Hampshire is uniquely tax-friendly for individuals: it has no general sales tax and no tax on wage income, and its longstanding Interest & Dividends Tax was fully repealed effective January 1, 2025 — so the state now has no personal income tax at all (as of 2026 — confirm current status). Businesses are the exception: New Hampshire levies a Business Profits Tax and a Business Enterprise Tax on companies above certain thresholds, so incorporated businesses still file at the state level (confirm current rates and thresholds before filing). There's also an 8.5% Meals & Rooms (Rentals) Tax on prepared food, lodging, and car rentals (as of 2026 — confirm current rate). For owners, that means personal-side simplicity but real attention needed on the business returns — exactly where good books pay off.
