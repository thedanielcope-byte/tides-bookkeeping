📍 About Florida

A State Built for Business.

Home to roughly 22 million residents — the 3rd-most populous state in the country — Florida has no state income tax, a tourism economy that's the envy of the world, and one of the highest small-business formation rates in America. From the Panhandle to the Keys, every region tells a different economic story.

🏙️
South Florida
Miami · Fort Lauderdale · West Palm Beach
Florida's economic powerhouse — Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties. Latin American banking and finance, the world's largest cruise port, luxury real estate, and a fast-growing tech and venture capital scene.
🎢
Central Florida
Orlando · Lakeland · Kissimmee
The theme-park capital of the world. Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando, and SeaWorld anchor a tourism, hospitality, and convention economy in Orange and Polk counties — supported by Publix's Lakeland HQ and a booming logistics corridor.
⚓
Tampa Bay & Suncoast
Tampa · St. Petersburg · Clearwater · Sarasota
Hillsborough, Pinellas, and Sarasota counties — a diversified economy of finance, healthcare, defense (MacDill AFB / CENTCOM), Port Tampa Bay, and one of the country's hottest small-business and relocation markets.
🚀
First Coast, Space Coast & Panhandle
Jacksonville · Cape Canaveral · Pensacola · Tallahassee
Jacksonville (Florida's largest city by area), JAXPORT and naval logistics, Kennedy Space Center / SpaceX on the Space Coast, plus the Panhandle's military bases (Eglin, NAS Pensacola) and the state capital in Tallahassee.

Florida's Economy & Business Climate

Florida is one of the most business-friendly states in America — with no state income tax, no estate tax, and a state government that has spent decades courting employers and retirees alike. The result is a magnetic effect: thousands of out-of-state businesses and high-income residents relocate to Florida every year, and the state's population continues to grow faster than nearly anywhere else in the country.

Florida became the 27th state in 1845 and is the only state that borders both the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico. Its modern economy is anchored by tourism (Disney World, Universal Orlando, Miami Beach), real estate and construction, agriculture (citrus, sugarcane, tomatoes), aerospace (Kennedy Space Center, SpaceX, Blue Origin), aviation, healthcare, international finance, and defense.

🎢
Tourism — $100B+ Annual Industry
Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando, SeaWorld, Miami Beach, Key West, and the Gulf Coast beaches collectively draw more than 130 million visitors a year — the engine behind FL hotels, restaurants, short-term rentals, and retail.
🛳️
PortMiami — World's Largest Cruise Port
Home base for Royal Caribbean and Carnival, PortMiami moves more cruise passengers than any port on Earth, while Port Tampa Bay and JAXPORT (Jacksonville) anchor Florida's container and bulk-cargo trade.
🚀
Space Coast — NASA, SpaceX & Blue Origin
Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral Space Force Station drive a booming aerospace cluster in Brevard County — SpaceX, Blue Origin, Lockheed Martin, and a deep supplier network of FL small businesses.
🪖
Military — MacDill, Eglin, NAS Pensacola, NAS Jacksonville
MacDill AFB (Tampa) hosts U.S. Central Command and SOCOM. Eglin AFB anchors the Panhandle. Combined with NAS Pensacola and Jacksonville's naval bases, Florida ranks among the top defense-spending states in the country.
🛒
Publix, Disney, NextEra & AdventHealth
Florida is home to Publix Super Markets (Lakeland HQ, employee-owned), Walt Disney World (the state's largest single-site employer), NextEra Energy / FPL (Juno Beach), and hospital giants AdventHealth and HCA Florida.

Key Interstate & Highway Infrastructure

Florida's highway network ties together a long, skinny state — moving freight, tourists, and snowbirds between the Panhandle, the peninsula, and the Keys. Combined with Florida's Turnpike, it's one of the busiest road networks in the South.

I-95 — Atlantic Coast Spine
Runs the length of Florida's east coast — Jacksonville, Daytona, the Space Coast, West Palm, Fort Lauderdale, and Miami. The freight and tourism backbone of South Florida.
I-75 — Gulf Coast & Alligator Alley
Tampa Bay down through Sarasota, Fort Myers, and Naples, then east across the Everglades on Alligator Alley to Fort Lauderdale — the artery of Southwest Florida growth.
I-4 — Orlando–Tampa Corridor
Connects Tampa to Orlando to Daytona Beach — the heart of Florida's tourism and logistics economy and one of the country's busiest interstates.
I-10 — Panhandle Route
Pensacola to Tallahassee to Jacksonville — the cross-Panhandle route linking Florida's military bases and the state capital to the east coast.
Florida's Turnpike
A 312-mile toll road running from Miami-Dade through Orlando and into Central Florida — the fastest north-south alternative to I-95.
US-1 — Overseas Highway
From Miami through the Florida Keys to Key West — 113 miles of bridges and causeways that drive the entire Keys tourism economy.

FL Landmarks & Cultural Identity

🏰
Walt Disney World & Universal Orlando
The most-visited tourist destinations on the planet — combined, they drive the bulk of Central Florida's economy and a massive ecosystem of vendor, hospitality, and contractor small businesses.
🎓
University of Florida, FSU & UM
UF (Gainesville), Florida State (Tallahassee), and the University of Miami anchor a higher-ed system of hundreds of thousands of students — fueling the state's healthcare, research, finance, and tech workforce.
🐊
Everglades & Florida Keys
Everglades National Park, the largest subtropical wilderness in the U.S., and the 120-mile Florida Keys archipelago — both globally recognized tourism, fishing, and outdoor-economy drivers.
