About the Area

Maui County:
Central Commerce,
Coastal Business.

Maui County spans the islands of Maui, Molokaʻi, and Lānaʻi, but its business heart is Central Maui — the Kahului–Wailuku core. Kahului is the island's commercial and industrial engine, home to the main airport, the deep-draft harbor, and the big-box and light-industrial base that supplies the whole island.

Wailuku, just inland, is the county seat and government center, with a historic Main Street small-business district. To the south, Kīhei anchors a busy tourism and small-business corridor. Maui's economy has evolved from its sugar and pineapple heritage into tourism, professional services, and a growing tech presence at the Maui Research & Technology Park — all operating under Hawaiʻi's General Excise Tax on gross income.

History & Economic Identity

Maui County built its early economy on sugar and pineapple — vast plantations across the central isthmus and upcountry slopes that shaped its towns, roads, and communities for a century. As agriculture wound down, tourism and services became the island's economic backbone, but the plantation heritage still shows in Central Maui's layout and in a strong agricultural and food-producing tradition that continues today.

Kahului is the commercial and industrial hub — anchored by Kahului Airport, the island's deep-draft harbor at Kahului Harbor, and the retail and light-industrial corridors along Dairy Road and Puʻunene Avenue. Neighboring Wailuku, the county seat, is the government center, its historic Main Street home to law firms, agencies, restaurants, and a growing creative small-business scene.

To the south, Kīhei runs a dense tourism and small-business corridor along the coast, with the Maui Research & Technology Park bringing a science and tech presence to the island. Across all of Central and South Maui — from a Kahului contractor to a Kīhei café — Hawaiʻi's General Excise Tax applies to gross business income, making disciplined monthly bookkeeping a real and recurring need for local owners.
