About the Area

Lowndes County Is
South Georgia's
Regional Hub.

Lowndes County is the commercial and cultural center of South Georgia, anchored by Valdosta -- a regional hub for healthcare, education, retail, and military operations. The county sits on I-75 just 15 miles north of the Florida border, making it a gateway between Georgia and Florida's panhandle region.

With Moody Air Force Base driving military spending, Valdosta State University supporting education and student services, and South Georgia Medical Center anchoring healthcare, Lowndes County has a stable and diversified economy. The county's agricultural roots -- timber, pecans, and cotton -- still underpin much of the rural economy.

History & Economic Identity

Lowndes County was created in 1825 and Valdosta was established as the county seat in 1860, named after the governor's estate Val d'Aosta. The county prospered through cotton, timber, and turpentine, and by the early 1900s Valdosta was one of the wealthiest small cities in the South.

Moody Air Force Base, established in 1941, has been a cornerstone of the county's economy for over 80 years. Home to the 23rd Wing and Combat Rescue operations, the base employs thousands of military and civilian personnel and generates hundreds of millions in annual economic impact. Valdosta State University adds another 11,000+ students and hundreds of faculty and staff.

Today, Lowndes County blends its military and university anchors with a growing healthcare sector (South Georgia Medical Center is the region's largest hospital) and a retail economy that serves as the shopping destination for much of South Georgia and North Florida. Agriculture remains important, with timber, pecans, and row crops sustaining the rural economy.
