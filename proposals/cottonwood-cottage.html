Bookkeeping Proposal

Nine properties, one venue, and nothing left on your desk

Prepared for Brent Knittel — Cottonwood Cottage Prepared by Dan Cope · Tides Bookkeeping October 8, 2026

Where you're starting

Your numbers are solid. The monthly grind is the problem.

You run three companies: the wedding venue, a Nevada Series LLC with nine rental properties, and the corporation that owns the venue building. You’ve kept separate books for every property for years, on spreadsheets built from bank downloads, and your CPA gets clean buckets at tax time. January through September of this year is already done.

So this isn’t a cleanup. What wears on you is the routine: 20 to 50 paper bills a month that you open, read, pay and file, a PO Box that fills up, and a catch-up session that gets heavier every month it slides. And because everything lives in spreadsheets, you can’t pull a profit and loss or a balance sheet without rebuilding it first.

You told me what you want: the mail and the bookkeeping off your desk, a report whenever you ask for one, and to never sort out a QuickBooks entry yourself again. You keep the final say on every payment.

How I'd set it up

Four pieces

01
One QuickBooks file for the properties, with a class for each one
The Series LLC goes in a single file on QuickBooks Plus. The master and each of the nine properties get their own class, and all ten bank accounts and the Visa connect to it. You run one report for the whole series, or pick a property and see only its rent and expenses.
02
The venue and the building company each keep their own file
The venue stays in its own QuickBooks, now connected to its bank account and card. On our call we talked about making the building company a class inside the venue file. I’d give it its own file on the smallest plan instead: it is a separate corporation with its own tax return, and twelve rent payments in and twelve mortgage payments out take very little to keep.
03
Rent and the Visa, recorded the way you already run them
Rent keeps arriving in the master account by Zelle. I record each payment to its property the day it lands, and your transfers out to the property accounts are tracked so every property squares up with the master. Visa charges are classed by property, and the Home Depot and Costco splits come from your download as one entry per period.
04
Mail scanned, and every bill checked against last month
Bills go to a staffed mail-scanning address near you in place of the PO Box. I file each scan to the right property by its service address and enter it. Paper was how you caught a bill that crept up, so I compare every recurring bill with the month before and flag anything that moves more than a percentage you choose, before it is paid.

Scope of work

What I’ll do

  • One-time setup (October). Build the properties file and the building company file, connect all the bank accounts and cards across the three companies, set up the classes and a chart of accounts that matches the buckets your CPA already uses, load each property’s balances as of September 30 from your spreadsheets, and get the mail and bill routine running. October’s bookkeeping is part of the setup.
  • Ongoing every month. Every account reconciled, every transaction recorded to its property, bills entered from the scans and queued for you, and each month closed with reports you can open in the portal. October through December is the practice run, so everything is dialed in by January 1, 2027.
  • In step with your CPA. For the 2026 return your CPA gets your January–September spreadsheets plus October–December from QuickBooks, by property. From 2027 it all comes from QuickBooks. I keep the books; your CPA handles the returns and any tax questions.
