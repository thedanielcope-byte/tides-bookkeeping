About the Area

The Fastest-Growing
Corner of Alaska -
By a Wide Margin.

The Matanuska-Susitna Borough is enormous - larger than West Virginia - and it is growing faster than anywhere else in Alaska. Palmer is the borough seat, Wasilla is the commercial center, and the fast-growing unincorporated areas in between (Knik-Fairview, Meadow Lakes, Lakes, Butte) now hold more people than either city.

A large share of the working population commutes down the Glenn Highway to Anchorage - which makes the Mat-Su Alaska's only real commuter belt and drives an economy built on construction, trades, retail, and services. If somebody is building a house in Alaska this year, there is a good chance it is here.

Underneath the growth sits an older identity: the Matanuska Colony farms of the 1930s, the Alaska State Fair, and an agricultural tradition that still gives the valley its character. The Mat-Su is where Alaska grows things - and, increasingly, where Alaska builds things.

History & Economic Identity

In 1935, at the depth of the Great Depression, the federal government relocated about 200 farm families from Michigan, Wisconsin, and Minnesota to the Matanuska Valley. The Matanuska Colony was a New Deal experiment - hand them 40 acres and see if Alaska could farm. It half worked, and it permanently stamped the valley with an agricultural identity that survives today in the barns still standing around Palmer and in the Alaska State Fair, where the giant-vegetable competition is a genuinely serious annual event.

The modern Mat-Su, though, is a growth story. As Anchorage ran out of buildable land and got expensive, the valley became where Alaskans went to buy a house - and the Glenn Highway became the commute that made it possible. The borough has grown faster than any other part of the state for decades, and the population is now heavily concentrated in unincorporated areas like Knik-Fairview and Meadow Lakes rather than in Wasilla or Palmer themselves.

For small businesses that means one thing above all: construction and the trades. New homes, new commercial buildings, new roads. Every framer, electrician, plumber, excavator, and roofer in Southcentral Alaska is busy here - and all of them are compressing a year's work into a build season that effectively closes when the ground freezes.

🏗️
The Construction Economy
The Mat-Su builds more than anywhere else in Alaska. General contractors, framers, electricians, plumbers, excavators, and roofers - all working a short season and all needing real job costing to survive it.
🚗
The Anchorage Commuter Belt
Tens of thousands of Mat-Su residents drive the Glenn Highway to Anchorage jobs. That means valley businesses serve a population that earns its money elsewhere - a retail and services dynamic unlike anywhere else in the state.
🌽
Matanuska Colony Agriculture
The 1935 New Deal colony gave the valley Alaska's only significant farming belt. Peonies, potatoes, and the famously enormous cabbages of the Alaska State Fair - plus a growing farm-to-table and agritourism trade.
🎡
The Alaska State Fair
Eleven days at the end of summer in Palmer, and one of the largest events in the state. For dozens of valley vendors, food operators, and crafters, the fair is a meaningful chunk of the year's revenue in a single burst.

💰 The Alaska Tax Angle

No State Sales Tax.
But Read This Anyway.

Alaska has no state sales tax and no state personal income tax — which is exactly why so many business owners get Alaska tax wrong. More than 100 local jurisdictions levy their own sales tax, and the rules change borough by borough and city by city.

Mat-Su Borough: The Numbers

The Mat-Su has one of the trickier sales tax setups in Alaska, and it catches contractors in particular. The borough itself levies no general sales tax - but two of its cities do, and whether you owe depends on exactly where the work happens.

Alaska State Sales Tax None
Alaska Personal Income Tax None
Borough General Sales Tax None
City of Wasilla Sales Tax 2.5% (as of 2026)
City of Palmer Sales Tax ~3% (as of 2026 - confirm)

Rates shown are as of 2026 and can change. Always confirm the current rate with the taxing jurisdiction before you rely on it. Tides keeps your books tax-ready and works alongside your CPA — we do not file returns for you.

What It Means for Your Books

📍
Your tax depends on the job site, not your office
The Matanuska-Susitna Borough levies no general sales tax. The City of Wasilla levies 2.5% and the City of Palmer levies roughly 3% (rates as of 2026 - confirm with each city, and note that Palmer has used temporary seasonal add-ons in the past). If you are a contractor headquartered in Meadow Lakes doing a job inside Wasilla city limits, the city rate can apply to that job even though your yard is in an untaxed area. Getting this wrong across dozens of jobs a year adds up fast.
🏗️
A build season that closes when the ground freezes
Mat-Su contractors compress a year of revenue into roughly May through October. That makes job costing non-negotiable: one underbid job is a much larger share of your year here than it would be somewhere with a twelve-month season. We track cost per job, keep subcontractor 1099s clean, and make sure materials bought in spring against a job that closes in September land in the right period.
🌐
Selling into the rest of Alaska? ARSSTC
If you ship or deliver into Alaska communities that do levy sales tax - Juneau, Sitka, Kodiak, the Kenai Peninsula - the Alaska Remote Sellers Sales Tax Commission is your single point of registration and filing. Threshold: $100,000 in gross statewide sales in the current or prior calendar year. The 200-transaction test was removed effective January 1, 2025.
